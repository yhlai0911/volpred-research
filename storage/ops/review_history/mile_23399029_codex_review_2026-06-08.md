## Findings

1. **The headline “5 historical crises” double-counts the same GFC event, so the aggregate ranking is not based on five distinct episodes.**  
   `experiments/k674/k674_crisis_scenarios.py:235-265` defines both `GFC (2008-09)` and `Slow Bear (2007-09 extended)`. Those windows overlap heavily and share the same 2008-2009 collapse. Then `k674_crisis_scenarios.py:373-409` averages drawdowns across all five scenarios as if they were equally independent observations. The article framing “五次歷史危機壓力測試” therefore overstates the breadth of evidence and implicitly gives the GFC shock extra weight in the final ranking.

2. **The “protection ratio” formula is mathematically inconsistent with the prose label “X% less drawdown.”**  
   `experiments/k674/k674_crisis_scenarios.py:459-460` computes `abs(best_avg_dd / worst_avg_dd) * 100` and prints it as `"% less drawdown"`. That expression is the retained drawdown ratio, not the reduction. Example: if the best strategy suffers 5% of the benchmark drawdown, the code prints `5% less drawdown`, when the correct reduction is `95% less drawdown`. Any article sentence derived from this summary would materially misstate effect size.

3. **The cost / implementation disclosure does not match the backtest mechanics in code.**  
   `experiments/k674/k674_crisis_scenarios.py:126-148` reweights exposure every trading day using previous-close VIX, but the saved limitations section says `No transaction costs (monthly rebalancing ~5-10 bps/year impact)` at `k674_crisis_scenarios.py:507-513`. This is not a harmless wording issue: daily VIX-based resizing and monthly rebalancing have different turnover, cost, and operational assumptions. The article should not present the current results as if the implementation-cost story were already settled.

## What Holds

1. **Lookahead audit PASS.**  
   `experiments/k674/k674_crisis_scenarios.py:130` uses `vix_series.loc[dates[i - 1]]`, so crisis-day returns are driven by the previous trading day's VIX, not same-day close information.

2. **Per-scenario NAV mechanics are internally coherent.**  
   `experiments/k674/k674_crisis_scenarios.py:126-148` applies lagged VIX weights to same-day SPY / GLD returns in a consistent way, and `k674_results.json` reflects those outputs.

## Conclusion

This article should not be treated as a clean “five-crisis” evidence piece in its current form. The source code supports a descriptive stress-test on overlapping crisis windows, but not the stronger published framing that implies five distinct historical crisis trials and a properly scaled aggregate protection statistic. Before reuse or republication, K674 should be revised to:

- separate distinct crises from alternate windows of the same crisis,
- recompute the aggregate ranking on non-overlapping episodes or explicitly label the weighting choice,
- fix the protection-ratio formula and any prose derived from it,
- align the transaction-cost disclosure with the actual daily reweighting implementation.

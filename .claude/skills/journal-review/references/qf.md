# Quantitative Finance (QF)

- **Abbrev / ISSN**: QF (Quant. Finance); ISSN 1469-7688 (print), 1469-7696 (online)
- **Publisher**: Taylor & Francis (Routledge). Submit via ScholarOne Manuscripts.
- **Tier**: Top-field quantitative finance (ABS/AJG 3, ABDC A, SCI/SSCI Q1 in Business/Finance and Q3 in Mathematics-Interdisciplinary Applications). The flagship methods-oriented journal bridging financial mathematics, computational finance, econometric methods for markets, and applied practice.

## Scope
Quantitative techniques applied to financial markets: derivatives pricing, risk management, portfolio construction, algorithmic trading, market microstructure, volatility modelling, machine-learning-in-finance, network models, systemic risk. Both theoretical (stochastic calculus, option pricing) AND empirical/computational papers welcome, but always with a quantitative methods lens.
- **Fits**: volatility model comparison with novel evaluation angle, derivative pricing with market data, portfolio optimization with new methods, HFT/microstructure with quantitative model, machine-learning-in-volatility, systemic risk network models.
- **Weak fit**: pure empirical asset pricing without quantitative-methods angle (→ JEmpFin/JBF), pure economics/behavioural finance (→ JF/JFE), narrow econometric methodology without finance application (→ JoE/JBES).

## Format
- **Length**: no hard cap; typical published papers 20-35 pages. Long derivations → online supplement.
- **Abstract**: unstructured, ~≤200 words typical (no strict count in guide; keep ≤150 to be safe).
- **Keywords/JEL**: 3-6 keywords required; JEL codes recommended.
- **Structure**: IMRaD or math-paper structure (introduction, model, results, empirical, conclusion); single-anonymized (author names visible to reviewers, reviewers anonymous to authors).
- **References**: Chicago author-date; DOIs where available.
- **Figures/tables**: editable; equations numbered; supplementary appendix for long proofs/extra tables.
- **LaTeX**: strongly preferred (math-heavy field); T&F provides QF-specific LaTeX template (`rquf20`).

## Submission process
ScholarOne Manuscripts. **No submission fee**. Required: manuscript (LaTeX preferred), abstract, 3-6 keywords, cover letter (short — journal is methods-oriented, not framing-heavy). Reviewers screened by two anonymous referees plus editor.

## Review model
Single-anonymized (author names visible to reviewers; reviewers anonymous to authors). Editor + AE screen → 2 referees → decision. First-decision time typically 2-4 months (estimate; unverified for 2025).

## Fees & OA
No submission fee. Hybrid: subscription route free; gold OA APC USD 3,175 (T&F 2025 rate, verify live). Read & Publish agreements waive APC at many institutions.

## Distinctive requirements
1. **Quantitative-methods lens is a HARD gate**: paper must showcase or apply a nontrivial quantitative technique (stochastic model, novel estimator, computational algorithm, ML pipeline). Straight-up empirical finance without a methods angle is a scope misfit.
2. **Single-anonymized review** — do NOT strip names; standard title/author block.
3. **LaTeX strongly preferred** — Word submissions may be accepted but math typesetting is graded by reviewers.
4. Supplementary material / code repository encouraged (not mandatory pre-2024, verify current policy).
5. Broad readership: audience includes practitioners at investment banks and hedge funds — writing should be technically precise but accessible.

## Pre-submission checklist (extends generic)
- Abstract ≤150 words, unstructured (safer than the ~200 nominal cap).
- 3-6 keywords + JEL codes on title page.
- **Not anonymized** (single-blind) — standard author/affiliation block.
- **Methods lens explicit in intro**: state the quantitative technique / model / algorithm in the first paragraph; empirical results support/demonstrate it.
- LaTeX manuscript using T&F `rquf20` template (or interchangeable Elsevier-style with clean math).
- Chicago author-date refs + DOIs.
- Code/data supplementary package prepared (reviewers may ask; increasingly expected).
- Econometric rigor when doing forecasting/eval: correct OOS design, lagged signals, DM/HLN/SPA + HAC, QLIKE = actual/predicted, cluster-robust inference.
- Multiple-testing control (Reality Check/SPA/Romano-Wolf/MCS) for any "significant improvement" claim.
- Robustness: sensitivity to model hyperparameters, alternative asset classes, subsample stability including a crisis/bear period.
- Position vs recent QF + adjacent journals (Mathematical Finance, Finance & Stochastics, IJTAF); state contribution as quantitative-methodological.
- No "only" or "unique" claim without re-verifying against current results.

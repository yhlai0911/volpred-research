\section{Issue H1: Circular Logic in $\gamma$--TSMOM Argument}
% =============================================================================

\noindent\textbf{Review concern (Issues 1.1 + 7.1):} Both $\gamma$ and the TSMOM loading are estimated from the same return series, creating mechanical endogeneity in the $N=22$ cross-sectional regression. The paper frames this as a ``predictive relationship'' without adequate acknowledgment of the identification challenge.

\subsection{Changes Made}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Split-sample robustness test (Section 3.2).}

A new paragraph ``Endogeneity concern and split-sample robustness'' is added after the main cross-sectional results. The test estimates $\gamma$ from the first half (2007--2016) and $\hat{\beta}_{\text{TSMOM}}^{\perp}$ from the second half (2017--2026) using non-overlapping data. Results:
\begin{itemize}
\item Split-sample Pearson $r = 0.487$ ($p = 0.021$), bootstrap 95\% CI $[0.114, 0.737]$
\item Split-sample Spearman $\rho = 0.461$ ($p = 0.031$)
\item Attenuation relative to full-sample ($r = 0.564$) is acknowledged and explained as expected given reduced estimation precision
\end{itemize}

The paragraph explicitly states that this approach ``reduces but does not fully eliminate the mechanical channel'' and discusses why a formal IV approach is infeasible with $N = 22$ mixed-class assets.

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Panel B in Table 2 (Cross-Sectional Predictors).}

Table~2 is expanded with a new Panel B reporting split-sample results alongside the existing full-sample Panel A, making the robustness check immediately visible to reviewers.

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Abstract updated.}

Added one sentence to the abstract: ``A split-sample robustness check---estimating $\gamma$ from 2007--2016 and TSMOM loading from 2017--2026---yields $r = 0.487$ ($p = 0.021$), confirming that the relationship is not purely mechanical.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Introduction Contribution 1 updated.}

Added: ``A potential endogeneity concern arises because both $\gamma$ and the TSMOM loading are estimated from the same return series: a split-sample robustness check (estimating $\gamma$ from 2007--2016 and TSMOM loading from 2017--2026) yields $r = 0.487$ ($p = 0.021$), confirming that the relationship survives temporal separation.''

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Limitations paragraph 5 (Section 4.4).}

New paragraph added: ``Fifth, the cross-sectional regression linking $\gamma$ to TSMOM loading ($N = 22$) faces an inherent endogeneity concern\ldots Future work with a larger cross-section of futures contracts---as in Hood \& Raughtigan's 50-asset sample---could employ a more diverse set of instruments or within-asset-class variation to address this limitation.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Conclusion updated.}

Added after the full-sample correlation: ``a split-sample robustness check ($r = 0.487$, $p = 0.021$) confirms this relationship survives temporal separation of the estimation windows.''

\end{enumerate}

\section{Issue H2: MDD Retention Lacks Statistical Test}
% =============================================================================

\noindent\textbf{Review concern (Issue 7.2):} The central claim (90--97\% MDD retention) is based on point estimates without confidence intervals. MDD is a single extreme-value statistic with high sampling variability.

\subsection{Changes Made}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{New Section 2.7: ``MDD Retention Bootstrap Inference.''}

A complete methodological description of the block bootstrap procedure:
\begin{itemize}
\item 10,000 replications, block size = 252 trading days
\item Formal definition of MDD retention for each bootstrap draw (new Eq.~\ref{eq:mdd_retention_boot} in the paper)
\item Reports 5th and 95th percentiles as 90\% confidence interval
\item Justification: block bootstrap preserves serial dependence and path-dependent nature of MDD
\end{itemize}

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{New Table 6 (``Block Bootstrap Confidence Intervals for MDD Retention'').}

\begin{center}
\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule
& SPY & 50/50 & DIA & QQQ & IWM \\
\midrule
Point estimate & 93\% & 96\% & 91\% & 90\% & 97\% \\
90\% CI lower & 86\% & 90\% & 83\% & 82\% & 91\% \\
90\% CI upper & 97\% & 99\% & 96\% & 95\% & 100\% \\
$H_0$: $\leq 80\%$ & Reject & Reject & Reject & Reject & Reject \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

All five lower bounds exceed 80\%. The one-sided test rejects $H_0$: Retention $\leq 80\%$ at the 1\% level for all five assets.

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Bootstrap interpretation paragraph (Section 3.3).}

New paragraph: ``Because maximum drawdown is a single extreme-value statistic with high sampling variability, we provide formal statistical inference via block bootstrap\ldots Even under conservative inference, at least four-fifths of VT's drawdown protection survives TSMOM removal for every asset tested.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Abstract updated.}

Added: ``Block bootstrap confidence intervals (10,000 replications, block size = 252 days) confirm that MDD retention is statistically robust, with 90\% CIs excluding values below 80\% for all five assets tested.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Introduction Contribution 2 updated.}

Added: ``Block bootstrap inference (10,000 replications, block size = 252 days) confirms that MDD retention is statistically robust: the 90\% confidence intervals are [86\%, 97\%] for SPY, [90\%, 99\%] for 50/50, [83\%, 96\%] for DIA, [82\%, 95\%] for QQQ, and [91\%, 100\%] for IWM, all excluding values below 80\%.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Table 3 notes updated.}

Added cross-reference: ``Block bootstrap 90\% confidence intervals for MDD retention are reported in Table~6.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Discussion Section 4.1 updated.}

Added parenthetical: ``with block bootstrap 90\% CIs excluding retention below 80\% for all five assets; Table~6.''

\item \MODIFIED\ \textbf{Conclusion updated.}

Added: ``with block bootstrap 90\% confidence intervals excluding retention below 80\% for all five assets.''

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Limitations paragraph 6 (Section 4.4).}

New paragraph: ``Sixth, our MDD retention estimates, while supported by block bootstrap inference (Table~6), are based on five US equity assets. Extending the MDD decomposition to the full 22-asset sample and to international markets would further strengthen the generalizability of the 90--97\% retention finding.''

\end{enumerate}

\section{Issue H4: Three Orphan Bibliography Entries}
% =============================================================================

\noindent\textbf{Review concern (Issues 3.1 + 10.1):} Three bibliography entries---\texttt{barroso2015}, \texttt{daniel2016}, \texttt{fleming2001}---appear in the bibliography but are never cited in the text. These orphan references may trigger a desk reject.

\subsection{Changes Made}

All three orphan entries are now cited in the text at locations where they are substantively relevant. No entries were removed.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Barroso \& Santa-Clara (2015) --- cited in Section 1 (Introduction).}

\textit{Location:} Paragraph 2, after discussing the leverage effect connection to VT.

\textit{Citation context:} ``This mechanical channel is well-documented: \citet{barroso2015} show that volatility-scaling momentum strategies eliminates momentum crashes, precisely because the leverage effect creates a natural hedge that scales down exposure when volatility spikes after losses.''

\textit{Rationale:} Barroso \& Santa-Clara (2015) directly demonstrates that volatility-scaling (the core of VT) interacts with momentum through the leverage effect---precisely the paper's central thesis. Citing them in the Introduction establishes that the VT--momentum connection has precedent.

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Daniel \& Moskowitz (2016) --- cited in Section 1 (Introduction, last paragraph before roadmap).}

\textit{Location:} ``Our paper contributes to several literatures'' paragraph.

\textit{Citation context:} ``Our finding that VT's embedded momentum exposure operates through the leverage effect channel complements \citet{daniel2016}, who show that momentum crashes are driven by precisely this leverage-effect-induced volatility clustering.''

\textit{Rationale:} Daniel \& Moskowitz (2016) directly links leverage-effect-induced volatility to momentum crashes, providing the theoretical bridge between VT and TSMOM that the paper exploits.

\item \ADDED\ \textbf{Fleming, Kirby \& Ostdiek (2001) --- cited in Section 1 (Introduction, last paragraph before roadmap).}

\textit{Location:} ``Our paper contributes to several literatures'' paragraph.

\textit{Citation context:} ``We also extend the `volatility-as-insurance' perspective of \citet{lai2026a} to a global setting, building on the foundational work of \citet{fleming2001}, who established the economic value of volatility timing in a portfolio context.''

\textit{Rationale:} Fleming et al.\ (2001) is the foundational paper on the economic value of volatility timing---the intellectual ancestor of the VT literature. Citing them grounds the paper's contribution in the established literature.

\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Verification}

After these additions, all 18 bibliography entries are cited at least once in the text. The orphan count drops from 3 to 0.

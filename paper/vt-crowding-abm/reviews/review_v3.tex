\item \MED\ \textbf{H4 $\to$ M: VT Sharpe ratio definition.}

\textbf{R2 finding}: The VT Sharpe ratio was never defined (zero risk-free rate, leverage via $w > 1$, endogeneity not stated).

\textbf{R3 status}: \PARTIAL

Section~2.4 (line 101) now includes a parenthetical definition: ``VT strategy Sharpe ratio (annualized mean excess return over risk-free rate divided by annualized standard deviation of daily returns).'' This is a welcome addition but remains incomplete:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]

  \item The fact that VT weights can exceed 1.0 (up to the 1.5 cap), implying leverage, is not acknowledged in the Sharpe context.

\end{itemize}

\textbf{Recommended fix}: Add a footnote to Table~1: ``The risk-free rate is zero in the simulation. VT weights can reach 1.5 (implicit leverage). At high $\phi$, VT trading endogenously affects the market return from which the Sharpe ratio is computed.''

%--- M4 ---
\item \MED\ \textbf{M4: Exogenous $\lambda$ bias direction not discussed.}

\textbf{R3 status}: \OPEN

The Limitations section (lines 248--250) discusses exogenous $\lambda$ and mentions that ``liquidity evaporates during stress as market makers widen spreads, which would amplify the feedback loop and potentially lower the tipping point.'' This is directionally correct but hedges with ``potentially.'' The direction of the bias is theoretically unambiguous: a stress-responsive $\lambda$ would make the feedback loop more severe.

\textbf{Recommended fix}: Change ``potentially lower the tipping point'' to ``would likely lower the tipping point, making the 50--70\% threshold reported here an upper bound.''

\medskip

\item \MED\ \textbf{M8: ``Below 5\%'' current VT adoption claim has no source.}

\textbf{R3 status}: \OPEN

The abstract (line 36) says ``estimated below 5\%'' and the Discussion (line 240) says ``current levels below 5\%,'' but neither provides a citation or derivation. This claim is central to the paper's ``safe for now'' conclusion.

\textbf{Recommended fix}: Add a footnote: ``Based on Cole (2017), short-volatility and vol-sensitive strategies manage approximately USD~2 trillion against a global equity market capitalization of roughly USD~100 trillion, implying an adoption rate of approximately 2\%.''

Line 56 groups Danielsson et al.\ (2012) with LeBaron (2006) under ``ABMs have proven effective for studying feedback-driven market dynamics.''

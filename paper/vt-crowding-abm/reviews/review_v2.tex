\subsection*{\textcolor{errorred}{Round~1 Serious Issues (5)}}

\begin{enumerate}

%--- H1 ---
\item \HIGH\ \textbf{H1 (IV-1): VIX equation mismatch---code uses asymmetric $\max(0,\cdot)$ on realized vol.}

\textbf{Round~1 finding}: The code computes \texttt{vix\_target = VIX\_MEAN + gamma * max(0, realized\_vol - 0.16)}, meaning the VIX target only \emph{increases} above $\bar{V}$ when realized vol exceeds 16\%; it never \emph{decreases} below $\bar{V}$ due to low realized vol. The paper's Eq.~(2) showed a symmetric response.

\textbf{Round~2 status}: \PARTIAL

The revised Eq.~(2) now reads:
\[
\text{VIX}_t = \max\!\Big(0,\; \text{VIX}_{t-1} + \kappa\big(\bar{V} + \gamma \cdot \Delta\sigma^{\text{real}}_t - \text{VIX}_{t-1}\big) + \eta_t\Big)
\]
The outer $\max(0,\ldots)$ is a \textbf{non-negativity floor} on the VIX level---a sensible addition, but \emph{not} the issue identified in Round~1. The core problem was that $\Delta\sigma^{\text{real}}_t$ is computed as $\max(0,\, \hat{\sigma}^{(20)}_t - 0.16)$ in the code (the asymmetric response to realized vol), yet the equation does not show this. Additionally, $\Delta\sigma^{\text{real}}_t$ remains \textbf{undefined}---there is no explicit formula for it anywhere in the paper.

\textbf{What is still needed}: Define $\Delta\sigma^{\text{real}}_t \equiv \max(0,\, \hat{\sigma}^{(20)}_t - 0.16)$ where $\hat{\sigma}^{(20)}_t$ is the annualized standard deviation of the most recent 20 daily returns, and add one sentence explaining the asymmetry: ``The $\max(0,\cdot)$ ensures that VIX responds only to \emph{elevated} realized volatility, consistent with the empirical asymmetry in implied-realized volatility dynamics (the variance risk premium is typically positive).''

\medskip

%--- H2 ---
\item \HIGH\ \textbf{H2 (I-1): Sensitivity analysis mischaracterized as ``$3\times3\times3$ grid.''}

\textbf{Round~1 finding}: The text described a ``$3\times3\times3$ sensitivity grid'' implying a full-factorial design (27 unique parameter combinations), when the actual design is one-at-a-time (OAT: 9 settings $\times$ 3 adoption levels).

\textbf{Round~2 status}: \PARTIAL

Section~2.4 (line 101) now correctly reads ``a one-at-a-time (OAT) sensitivity analysis.'' However, two other locations were \textbf{not updated}:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Abstract} (line 36): still says ``Sensitivity analysis across 27 parameter combinations'' without the OAT qualifier.
  \item \textbf{Section 3.5} (line 191): ``producing 27 parameter combinations tested at $\phi \in \{10\%, 30\%, 50\%\}$'' still reads as if these are 27 unique parameter settings. The actual count is 9 OAT parameter settings $\times$ 3 adoption levels = 27 cells, not 27 unique parameter combinations.
  \item \textbf{Conclusion} (line 269): ``27 parameter combinations'' repeated without OAT context.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{What is still needed}: In all three locations, replace ``27 parameter combinations'' with ``27 OAT sensitivity cells (9 parameter settings $\times$ 3 adoption levels)'' or similar clarifying language. Add a sentence in the Limitations section acknowledging that interaction effects (e.g., high-$\lambda$ combined with high-$\gamma$) are not tested.

\textbf{Severity downgrade}: Since the core Section~2.4 is now correct, this is downgraded from HIGH to \MED.

\medskip

%--- H3 ---
\item \HIGH\ \textbf{H3 (III-1): Missing critical ABM/VT citations.}

\textbf{Round~1 finding}: The paper cites only 11 references despite engaging with 4 literatures (VT, crowding, ABM, market microstructure). Missing: LeBaron (2006), Farmer \& Foley (2009), Cederburg et al.\ (2020), Danielsson et al.\ (2012), Kinlaw et al.\ (2019), Hommes (2006).

\textbf{Round~2 status}: \OPEN

The only reference added was Cole (2017) for the \$2T attribution fix. The reference count increased from 11 to 12. None of the six ABM/VT citations recommended in Round~1 were added:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item LeBaron (2006, \emph{Handbook of Computational Economics})---the standard ABM finance survey---\textbf{not cited}.
  \item Farmer \& Foley (2009, \emph{Nature})---the canonical advocacy for ABM in economics---\textbf{not cited}.
  \item Cederburg et al.\ (2020, \emph{Journal of Financial Economics})---comprehensive OOS test of VT---\textbf{not cited}.
  \item Danielsson et al.\ (2012, \emph{Journal of Financial Economics})---procyclical leverage / endogenous risk---\textbf{not cited}.
  \item Kinlaw et al.\ (2019, \emph{Journal of Portfolio Management})---quantitative crowding measures---\textbf{not cited}.
  \item Hommes (2006)---heterogeneous agent models survey---\textbf{not cited}.
\end{enumerate}

A paper with 12 references that spans ABM, VT, crowding, and market microstructure literatures will be rejected at any serious finance journal purely on the basis of incomplete literature engagement. Reviewers will view this as a failure to engage with the field.

\textbf{What is still needed}: Add at minimum LeBaron (2006), Farmer \& Foley (2009), and Cederburg et al.\ (2020). Target $\geq$ 20 references. \textbf{This remains HIGH severity.}

\medskip

%--- H4 ---
\item \HIGH\ \textbf{H4 (IV-2): VT Sharpe ratio definition unclear.}

\textbf{Round~1 finding}: The VT Sharpe ratio is computed from levered portfolio returns ($w^{\text{VT}}_t \cdot r_t$, with weights up to 1.5) with zero risk-free rate, weighting total market returns that are themselves affected by VT trading. None of this is stated.

\textbf{Round~2 status}: \OPEN

No definition paragraph was added. The Sharpe ratio first appears in Table~1 without any formal definition. The three concerns from Round~1 are all unaddressed:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
  \item Risk-free rate is zero---not stated.
  \item VT weights applied to total market return (circularity at high $\phi$)---not discussed.
  \item Leverage via weights $> 1.0$---not acknowledged.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{What is still needed}: Add to Section~2 or as a footnote to Table~1: ``The VT Sharpe ratio is $\bar{r}^{\text{VT}} / \sigma^{\text{VT}}$ where $r^{\text{VT}}_t = w^{\text{VT}}_t \cdot r_t$. The risk-free rate is zero in the simulation. Weights can exceed 1.0 (up to the 1.5 cap), implying leverage. At high $\phi$, VT agents' own trading substantially influences $r_t$, making the Sharpe ratio endogenous to the adoption level.''

\textbf{Severity downgrade}: From HIGH to \MED---this is an exposition gap, not a methodological error.

\medskip

\textbf{Verdict: Minor--Major Revision (improved from Major Revision)}

The revision addressed the most serious factual error (USD~2T attribution) and made partial progress on the VIX equation and sensitivity analysis description. However, the two most impactful issues for journal acceptance---the undefined $\Delta\sigma^{\text{real}}_t$ in the model's core equation and the thin reference list---remain fully open.

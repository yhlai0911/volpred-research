\title{When Does Better Volatility Measurement Improve Allocation? A Pre-Specified Out-of-Sample Evaluation}

\begin{abstract}
\noindent
A large literature refines volatility models---adding asymmetry, regime
dependence, and fat tails---on the presumption that a better in-sample fit
carries through to better decisions. We ask whether refined volatility
measurement actually improves portfolio allocation, and answer it with a
pre-specified out-of-sample evaluation that carries each forecast through to
a volatility-targeting allocation, across seven primary assets spanning
equities, precious metals, government bonds, and cryptocurrency, extended to
twenty-six assets. The answer is largely no. After correcting for multiple
testing, asymmetric model selection yields no robust out-of-sample forecast
superiority over a symmetric benchmark; the cross-asset inverted-leverage
signal is weak and statistically indistinguishable from zero; and the one
durable benefit of volatility targeting---drawdown reduction---is governed by
an asset's base volatility level, not by its asymmetric structure. Our
contribution is a reproducible measurement-to-allocation evaluation framework
and the honest null it yields: in-sample refinement rarely survives an
out-of-sample decision test.
\end{abstract}

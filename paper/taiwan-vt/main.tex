% ─── Abstract ────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We examine volatility targeting (VT) strategies in the Taiwan stock market using the FTSE TWSE Taiwan 50 Index ETF (0050.TW) over 2008--2026. Three contributions emerge. First, the broad TAIEX exhibits a substantially stronger diversification amplification of the leverage effect (4.6$\times$ for $\sim$900 stocks; 1.45$\times$ for the investable 0050.TW, 50 stocks) compared to the U.S. market (2.8$\times$), attributable to higher correlation asymmetry among Taiwan-listed stocks during market declines. Despite the strong index-level leverage effect ($\gamma = 0.272$, $t = 3.18$), GJR-GARCH does not significantly improve QLIKE over standard GARCH (DM $p = 0.86$), yet GJR-based VT produces meaningfully higher Sharpe ratios (+0.114)---suggesting that tail-specific forecast accuracy, rather than average accuracy, drives VT performance. Second, a simple EWMA-based VT strategy improves risk-adjusted returns (Sharpe 0.729 $\rightarrow$ 0.796) while halving maximum drawdown ($-41.3\%$ $\rightarrow$ $-18.4\%$), with Student-$t$ VaR achieving a 0.5\% violation rate (Basel III Green Zone). Third, we document a persistent information transmission channel from U.S. to Asian equity markets and establish that the Asian opening auction is a highly efficient mechanism for cross-market price discovery: a 10-day SPY momentum signal predicts next-day 0050.TW close-to-close returns ($r = 0.376$, Sharpe 1.47, $t = 3.76$), but approximately 78\% of this alpha is absorbed by the overnight opening gap before any trader can act, yielding an open-to-open Sharpe of only 0.87 ($t = 2.22$). This c2c--o2o gap, confirmed across six Asia-Pacific markets, quantifies the speed of cross-market price discovery and demonstrates that call auction mechanisms efficiently aggregate pre-market information. Additionally, we show that the month-over-month change in Taiwan's leading indicator composite is the strongest local macro predictor of realized volatility ($t = 3.74$, $R^2 = 7.1\%$), and that combining VIX scaling with leading indicator direction significantly improves the VIX-only strategy (full-sample Sharpe 0.999 $\rightarrow$ 1.151, DM $p = 0.0005$). The pure $8.63/\text{VIX}$ strategy achieves a corrected Sharpe ratio of 0.690 over 2016--2026 with monthly rebalancing and lagged weights. VT results are robust to TSMC concentration: both TSMC-only and ex-TSMC portfolios exhibit positive VT benefits, with the diversified index showing a stronger leverage effect than its dominant constituent. These results demonstrate that VT benefits extend to emerging Asian markets and that cross-market information dynamics, while not exploitable as a trading strategy, reveal efficient price discovery mechanisms in time-zone-separated markets.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} volatility targeting, Taiwan stock market, leverage effect, diversification amplification, GARCH, cross-market price discovery, opening auction efficiency, leading indicators, VaR

\medskip

\end{abstract}

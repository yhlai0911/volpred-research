# Working Abstract (target: 150 words)

> **Draft — not for submission. Revise after body.tex is finalized.**

---

We document a robust earnings-announcement effect in conditional equity volatility using a multiplicative GARCH framework that separates firm-specific (g) from market-level (τ) variance components. Pooling 31 Taiwan TWSE bluechip stocks over 2010–2025, we estimate a shared τ coefficient on a binary earnings-announcement indicator of θ̂_EAV = +6.36 × 10⁻⁵ (cluster-bootstrap t = +5.24; placebo distance 13.6σ). The effect survives five robustness layers including drop-one-stock stability and multi-horizon EAV definitions. Cross-market out-of-sample validation on 30 U.S. S&P 500 large-caps confirms the magnitude: panel Diebold-Mariano t = −5.58 (Harvey-adjusted |t| > 3.0). A PCA-based market-factor absorption test (K1149) rules out the alternative that announcements merely cluster in market-stress episodes; θ_EAV survives in both markets (Scenario A). We conclude that earnings-day volatility amplification is a universal firm-event constant, best captured by a binary indicator rather than the analyst-surprise magnitude.

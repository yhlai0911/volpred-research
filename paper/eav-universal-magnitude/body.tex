\begin{abstract}
\noindent
We document a robust cross-market regularity in earnings-announcement volatility
amplification using a multiplicative GARCH framework that decomposes conditional
variance into a firm-specific GJR component ($g_{i,t}$) and a pooled market-level
factor ($\tau_t$). Estimating a shared $\tau$ coefficient on a binary earnings
indicator across three independent equity markets---Taiwan (N=31 TWSE
bluechips), the United States (N=30 S\&P 500 large-caps), and Japan (N=30 TOPIX
large-caps), all 2014--2025---we obtain $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}} > 0$ with
cluster-bootstrap $|t|$ of 5.24 (TW), 4.50 (US), and 11.99 (JP), all surviving
Bonferroni adjustment for the three-market joint test ($|t|> 2.39$). Within-stock
permutation placebos (n=60 per market) reject the null at 0/60 in every market,
with observed $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ standing 13.3 (TW), 70.7 (US), and
38.6 (JP) placebo standard errors above zero. Point-estimate magnitudes are
market-specific and structurally ordered: US $(1.91\times10^{-4})>$ JP
$(1.41\times10^{-4})>$ TW $(6.36\times10^{-5})$, consistent with cross-market
differences in analyst coverage density, earnings-call culture, and institutional
pre-announcement positioning. A PCA-based market-factor absorption test confirms
the effect is orthogonal to systematic stress in both markets (Scenario A), with
stress-interaction asymmetric across markets (US amplified; TW null). Within each
market, however, no observable firm-attribute predictor of $\theta_{\mathrm{EAV}}$
survives multiple-testing correction across four pre-registered tests. This
within-market null supports interpreting $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ as a
market-level constant whose cross-market variation reflects structural rather than
idiosyncratic forces.

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} earnings announcements, volatility amplification, multiplicative
GARCH, cross-market regularity, pooled panel estimation, cluster bootstrap

\medskip
\noindent
\textbf{JEL Classification:} C23, C58, G12, G14, M41
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%==============================================================

Corporate earnings announcements are among the most information-dense events in
equity markets. The observation that realized volatility spikes around
announcement dates---documented as early as \citet{beaver1968} for return
variance and \citet{patell1976} for corporate forecast behavior---has become a
textbook stylized fact in market microstructure and empirical asset pricing.
Yet two fundamental questions remain underexplored. First, to what extent is
earnings-announcement volatility amplification (EAV) a \emph{universal,
cross-market regularity}, as opposed to a phenomenon confined to well-studied
U.S. large-caps? Second, if EAV is universal in sign, is its \emph{magnitude}
also constant across markets, or does it reflect market-specific institutional
structure?

This paper answers both questions with a unified methodology applied
simultaneously to three large-cap equity markets. Our central finding is that
EAV is both universal in sign---positive and highly significant in Taiwan, the
United States, and Japan---and systematically ordered in magnitude, with a
market ranking (US $>$ JP $>$ TW) that closely tracks institutional features
such as analyst coverage density and earnings-disclosure culture. The key
analytical vehicle is a multiplicative GARCH framework adapted from
\citet{engle_rangel2008} and \citet{engle2013}, in which conditional variance
decomposes into a firm-specific short-run component $g_{i,t}$ and a pooled
market-level long-run component $\tau_t$ driven by observable covariates
including a binary earnings indicator.

The Bonferroni threshold for three simultaneous tests is $|t| > 2.39$ at the
5\% joint level. All three markets clear this threshold by substantial margins.
Under the \citet{harvey2016} threshold of $|t| > 3.0$, which accounts for
pervasive multiple-testing in the finance literature, Taiwan and Japan clear
comfortably; the U.S. estimate, at $t = 4.50$, also clears.

\subsection*{The Null Heterogeneity Contribution}

A key substantive contribution of the paper concerns what we \emph{do not} find:
no observable firm-attribute predictor of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$---sector,
market capitalization, beta, earnings frequency, trading volume, momentum, or
rolling temporal trend---survives multiple-testing correction in four
pre-registered within-market tests (K1109, K1113, K1114/K1140). This within-market
null result is not a weakness. Rather, it is the logical support for the
magnitude-ordering interpretation: if $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ behaved as
a firm-level idiosyncratic parameter, the cross-market comparison would be
confounded by sample-composition differences. The null heterogeneity evidence
supports treating $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ as a market-level constant,
allowing us to attribute the US$>$JP$>$TW ordering to market-level institutional
structure.

\subsection*{Relation to the Literature}

Our paper contributes to three strands of research. First, the earnings-event
literature documents that both trading volume and return variance spike around
announcement dates \citep{beaver1968, patell1976, patell_wolfson1979,
ball_kothari1991}. Our contribution is to show this effect survives in a
multiplicative GARCH framework estimated across three distinct markets, with
magnitude ordering rather than a single stylized fact.

Second, our multiplicative specification follows \citet{engle_rangel2008} and
\citet{engle2013}, who separate conditional variance into short-run and long-run
(or permanent and transitory) components. We adapt this framework by replacing
the macroeconomic long-run driver with an event-date covariate, preserving the
multiplicative structure's identifying power while focusing on the
announcement-day spike.

Third, we contribute to the literature on volatility regularities by documenting,
to our knowledge, the first directly comparable cross-market estimate of
earnings-announcement volatility amplification under a single unified model
specification. While the within-market EAV spike is well established
\citep{beaver1968,patell1976,patell_wolfson1979,ball_kothari1991}, we are not
aware of a prior study that estimates the EAV magnitude jointly across multiple
international equity markets with a common pooled-MLE design; the cross-country
earnings literature has instead focused on information content and disclosure
quality rather than on a comparable volatility-amplification coefficient. Our
evidence of magnitude ordering across Taiwan, the United States, and Japan therefore
extends the EAV regularity to a setting that is rarely studied jointly with a
unified model specification.

\section{Model}
\label{sec:model}
%==============================================================

\subsection{Multiplicative GARCH-EAV Specification}

Let $r_{i,t}$ denote the continuously compounded daily return for stock $i$ on
day $t$. We decompose conditional variance multiplicatively into a stock-specific
short-run GARCH component $g_{i,t}$ and a pooled market-level factor $\tau_t$:
%
\begin{equation}
\sigma^2_{i,t} = g_{i,t}\bigl(\omega_i, \alpha_i, \gamma_i, \beta_i\bigr)
                 \times \tau_t,
\label{eq:mult}
\end{equation}
%
where $g_{i,t}$ follows a GJR-GARCH(1,1) process:
%
\begin{equation}
g_{i,t} = \omega_i + \alpha_i \varepsilon^2_{i,t-1}
          + \gamma_i \varepsilon^2_{i,t-1} \mathbf{1}[\varepsilon_{i,t-1}<0]
          + \beta_i g_{i,t-1},
\label{eq:gjr}
\end{equation}
%
and $\varepsilon_{i,t} = r_{i,t} / \sqrt{\sigma^2_{i,t}}$ are standardized
innovations assumed standard normal.

The market-level factor $\tau_t$ is driven by observable covariates:
%
\begin{equation}
\tau_t = \max\!\left(
  \theta_0 + \theta_{\mathrm{VIX}} \cdot \mathrm{VIX}^2_{t-1}
           + \theta_{\mathrm{EAV}} \cdot \mathrm{EAV}_{i,t-1},\; \varepsilon
\right),
\label{eq:tau}
\end{equation}
%
where $\mathrm{EAV}_{i,t} = 1$ if firm $i$ announces earnings on day $t$
(else 0), and $\varepsilon > 0$ is a small floor ensuring positivity.
$\mathrm{VIX}_{t-1}^2$ enters as a global stress control, following the
specification hierarchy in \citet{engle2013}.\footnote{%
  The VIX squared rather than VIX level follows the variance--standard-deviation
  scale matching in \citet{engle_rangel2008}, which is standard in the GARCH-X
  literature.}

The parameter of primary interest is $\theta_{\mathrm{EAV}}$: the pooled
earnings-announcement effect on the market-level variance factor, shared
across all stocks in the estimation panel. A positive and significant
$\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ indicates that announcement events systematically
elevate the common market-level variance component, over and above the stock's
own GJR dynamics captured by $g_{i,t}$.

\subsection{Identification}

The decomposition in \eqref{eq:mult} identifies $\theta_{\mathrm{EAV}}$
from two sources of variation. Within any given stock $i$, announcement days
generate abnormal variance increments that cannot be attributed to prior-day
returns or to the VIX-driven long-run level; this within-stock, within-time
variation isolates the event indicator. Across stocks, the pooling constraint
that $\theta_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ is common to all $i$ disciplines the estimate
against the stock-level noise that would afflict single-firm GARCH-EAV
specifications.

An important concern is whether the earnings announcement date that enters
$\tau_t$ through $\mathrm{EAV}_{i,t-1}$ is genuinely exogenous conditional on
$g_{i,t}$ and $\mathrm{VIX}_{t-1}$. Earnings announcement dates are disclosed
weeks or months in advance \citep[see, e.g.,][]{patell1976} and are
administratively determined; they are not selected in response to realized
or anticipated $\tau_t$ innovations. We therefore treat the lagged indicator
$\mathrm{EAV}_{i,t-1}$ as predetermined, consistent with standard practice in
the earnings-event literature.

For Taiwan, earnings announcements follow a batch-disclosure convention: firms
release quarterly reports by a regulatory deadline, with the actual public
announcement typically arriving after trading hours. This implies the relevant
volatility shock materializes on $t+1$ (the first post-announcement trading day).
We index the event as $\mathrm{EAV}_{i,t-1}=1$ on the first trading day
following the announcement, so the lag structure is correctly aligned across all
three markets.\footnote{%
  For the U.S. and Japan, earnings calls typically occur during intraday or
  after-hours sessions; the $t-1$ lag similarly positions the indicator at the
  first full-day response window. K1060 documents the T+1 timing for Taiwan;
  K1147 and K1150 verify consistent timing for U.S. and Japan.}

\subsection{Estimation}

We estimate the model by pooled maximum likelihood (MLE) with stock-level fixed
effects $m_i$ (scale normalizations). The log-likelihood for the panel is:
%
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}(\theta) = -\frac{1}{2} \sum_{i=1}^{N} \sum_{t=1}^{T}
  \left[ \log \sigma^2_{i,t}(\theta) + \frac{r^2_{i,t}}{\sigma^2_{i,t}(\theta)} \right],
\label{eq:ll}
\end{equation}
%
where $\theta = (\omega_i, \alpha_i, \gamma_i, \beta_i, \theta_0,
\theta_{\mathrm{VIX}}, \theta_{\mathrm{EAV}})$. Optimization uses an
outer EM-style loop that alternates between updating per-stock GJR parameters
(given $\tau_t$) and updating pooled $\tau$ parameters (given $g_{i,t}$),
with inner \texttt{scipy.minimize} (L-BFGS-B). Convergence is declared when
the outer-loop parameter change $\|\Delta\theta\| < 10^{-7}$ and the
log-likelihood improvement $|\Delta\mathcal{L}| < 10^{-7}$.\footnote{%
  The inner L-BFGS-B optimizer reports \texttt{converged = False} for the
  Taiwan and U.S. fits, reflecting the extremely small magnitude of
  $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}} \sim 10^{-4}$--$10^{-5}$ relative to the
  BFGS gradient tolerance. Library-level convergence flags are sensitive to
  parameter scale in low-magnitude estimation problems; our outer-loop criterion is satisfied
  and the log-likelihood plateau is monotone. Japan reports \texttt{converged = True}.
  The replication package documents this explicitly, and Appendix~\ref{app:gradient}
  provides an analytic-gradient verification.}

\subsection{Inference: Cluster Bootstrap}

We conduct inference via cluster bootstrap with $B = 150$ replications, sampling
stocks with replacement. This accounts for cross-sectional dependence across
stocks sharing the same announcement calendar and market-wide $\tau_t$ dynamics.
The cluster-bootstrap $t$-statistic is:
%
\begin{equation}
t_{\mathrm{CB}} = \frac{\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}}{\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CB}}},
\label{eq:t_cb}
\end{equation}
%
where $\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CB}}$ is the bootstrap standard deviation
of the refit $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ across replications. The 95\%
confidence interval is the 2.5th--97.5th percentile of the bootstrap distribution.
We follow \citet{harvey2016} and use $|t_{\mathrm{CB}}| > 3.0$ as the primary
significance threshold, with $|t_{\mathrm{CB}}| > 2.39$ as the Bonferroni
threshold for $k = 3$ simultaneous markets.

\subsection{Placebo Permutation Test}

As an additional robustness check, we conduct within-stock permutation placebos.
For each of $n_{\mathrm{placebo}} = 60$ replications, the earnings-announcement
dates for each stock are randomly shuffled within that stock's own time series,
preserving the number of announcements. The model is refit on the permuted data,
yielding a placebo distribution of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ under the null
hypothesis that announcement timing is uninformative. We report the one-sided
rejection rate (fraction of placebos with $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}} \geq$
observed) and the $z$-score of the observed estimate relative to the placebo
distribution.

\label{sec:data}

\subsection{Equity Returns}

We use daily closing price data for three panels of large-cap stocks, all
obtained from Yahoo Finance via the \texttt{yfinance} Python library with
\texttt{auto\_adjust=False} to preserve unadjusted close prices. Data snapshots
are stored as Parquet files in the replication package; the reproduce script
reads local snapshots exclusively.

\begin{description}
\item[Taiwan (TW)] $N = 31$ TWSE bluechip stocks, daily 2014--2025 (pooled
  observations = 121,014). Tickers are selected from the top-capitalization
  constituents of the Taiwan 50 Index (0050.TW), covering financials,
  technology, materials, and consumer sectors. Data directory:
  \texttt{experiments/k1145/data/}.

\item[United States (US)] $N = 30$ S\&P 500 large-caps, daily 2014--2025.
  Tickers include blue-chip names spanning technology (AAPL, MSFT, NVDA, META,
  GOOGL), healthcare (JNJ, MRK, UNH, ABT, TMO), financials (JPM, MA, V),
  consumer (AMZN, COST, WMT, MCD, PEP, KO), and energy/industrials (CVX, XOM,
  HD). Data directory: \texttt{experiments/k1147/data/}.

\item[Japan (JP)] $N = 30$ TOPIX large-caps, daily 2014--2025. Tickers include
  leading names across automotive (7203\_T, 7267\_T), electronics (6758\_T,
  6861\_T, 8035\_T), financials (8306\_T, 8316\_T, 8411\_T), and trading
  houses (8001\_T, 8002\_T, 8031\_T, 8058\_T). Data directory:
  \texttt{experiments/k1150/data/}.
\end{description}

\subsection{Global Volatility Control: VIX}

We use the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX, ticker \texttt{\^{}VIX}) as the global
stress control entering $\tau_t$. Daily VIX values are obtained alongside the
equity data. VIX is common to all three estimation panels; using a common global
measure ensures that any cross-market difference in $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$
is not attributable to differences in the VIX scaling of the $\tau_t$ component.

\subsection{Earnings Announcement Dates}

\begin{description}
\item[Taiwan] Earnings announcement dates are compiled from a hand-collected
  announcement date file (\texttt{財報公告日.txt}), covering fiscal quarters
  for 2014--2025. Taiwan listed companies are required to file quarterly
  financial statements by regulatory deadlines set by the Financial Supervisory
  Commission; the announced date corresponds to the actual filing or release
  date. Following K1060, announcement effects are measured at $T+1$ (the first
  full trading day after the announcement), as Taiwan reporting typically occurs
  after market close.

\item[United States] Earnings announcement dates are sourced from
  \texttt{earnings\_dates.json}, populated via yfinance's
  \texttt{Ticker.earnings\_dates} API for the period 2014--2025. U.S. companies
  report quarterly; the event day is the announcement date as recorded in the
  yfinance database, typically aligned with the first market open following the
  earnings call or press release.

\item[Japan] Earnings announcement dates follow the same yfinance-sourced
  \texttt{earnings\_dates.json} format, covering TOPIX large-caps 2014--2025.
  Japanese listed companies typically report quarterly (with some semi-annual
  schedules); fiscal year alignment varies across firms.
\end{description}

\subsection{Robustness: Multi-Window, Drop-Stock, BH-FDR}

\textbf{Multi-window robustness.} We re-estimate the model with
$\mathrm{EAV}_{i,t-1}$ expanded to cover the two days surrounding the
announcement (window 3, i.e., $[-1,+1]$) and a five-day window
($[-2,+2]$). Table~\ref{tab:robustness} reports all results; for Taiwan:
Window-3 yields $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}} = 3.80 \times 10^{-5}$
($t_{\mathrm{Hess}} = 14.25$); window-5 yields $1.73 \times 10^{-5}$
($t_{\mathrm{Hess}} = 10.12$).
% source: experiments/k1145/k1145_results.json: robustness_eav_window.window_3.theta_eav
% source: experiments/k1145/k1145_results.json: robustness_eav_window.window_5.theta_eav
The monotonic decay from window-1 ($6.36 \times 10^{-5}$) to window-3
($3.80 \times 10^{-5}$) to window-5 ($1.73 \times 10^{-5}$) is
consistent with a genuine announcement-day spike that attenuates as
adjacent non-event days dilute the earnings signal.

\textbf{Drop-stock stability.} In five independent draws of 5 stocks
(seeds 42--46), all 26-stock subpanels return significant positive
$\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ in the range $[6.21, 7.96] \times 10^{-5}$
with Hessian $t \in [12.17, 14.12]$.

the two primary tests (pooled Hessian Wald + cluster bootstrap), both
remain at BH-adjusted $p = 0.0$, as the raw $p$-values from either
inferential channel are numerically indistinguishable from zero.

\subsection{Magnitude Comparison and Structural Ordering}

The three point estimates exhibit a clear ordering:
\[
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{US}} = 1.91\times10^{-4}
\;>\;
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{JP}} = 1.41\times10^{-4}
\;>\;
\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{TW}} = 6.36\times10^{-5}.
\]
The US-to-TW ratio is approximately 3:1; the JP-to-TW ratio is
approximately 2.2:1. We interpret this ordering through the lens of
cross-market institutional features.

\textbf{Analyst coverage density.} U.S. S\&P 500 constituents attract
the highest analyst coverage globally, generating concentrated
information-processing activity around earnings dates. Dense analyst
activity amplifies volatility both pre-announcement (as analysts revise
estimates) and post-announcement (as models update). Japanese TOPIX
large-caps also attract substantial international institutional coverage.
Taiwan TWSE bluechips operate in a smaller institutional investor
environment with less intensive sell-side coverage relative to market size.

\textbf{Earnings-call culture.} U.S. and, increasingly, Japanese listed
companies hold structured earnings conference calls with Q\&A sessions
open to analysts and institutional investors; these calls generate
additional information disclosure and re-evaluation. Taiwanese companies
typically release written reports without live calls of comparable scope,
reducing the information-processing intensity around the announcement.

\textbf{Institutional pre-announcement positioning.} Active positioning
around earnings events by institutional investors concentrates order flow,
amplifying the variance response. The U.S. options market, which explicitly
prices earnings-day volatility jumps \citep{patell_wolfson1979}, further
concentrates and amplifies variance responses.

The structural US$>$JP$>$TW magnitude ordering is internally consistent
because, as we document below, within-market firm-attribute heterogeneity
is null: $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ is a market-level constant within
each country, making the cross-market comparison clean.

\subsection{Reconciliation with Null Within-Market Heterogeneity}
\label{sec:nullhet}

A potential concern is that the cross-market magnitude ordering reflects
sample-composition differences (e.g., TW sample includes more
low-coverage firms) rather than market-level structure. The null
heterogeneity evidence chain addresses this concern directly.

\textbf{K1109: Pre-registered sector ANOVA (TW).} A one-way ANOVA of
firm-level $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV},i}$ (from single-firm GARCH fits)
across 8 TWSE sectors yields a joint $F = 1.31$, $p = 0.297$
(pre-registered; sector labels locked before estimation).
% source: experiments/k1109/k1109_results.json: anova_sector_ftest.f_stat = 1.3085
% source: experiments/k1109/k1109_results.json: anova_sector_ftest.p_value = 0.2968
No sector dummy survives BH-FDR correction (minimum BH-adjusted
$p = 0.278$ for the fabless sector).
% source: experiments/k1109/k1109_results.json: bh_adjusted_pvalues: min = sector_fabless = 0.278

\textbf{K1113: Six-covariate panel (TW).} We regress firm-level
$\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV},i}$ on six pre-registered covariates
(log market cap, rolling beta vs.\ 0050, earnings frequency per year,
log average volume, price volatility, industry momentum). All five
pre-registered hypotheses fail:
\begin{itemize}
\item H1 (any BH-survivor): minimum BH-adjusted $p = 0.854$; zero survivors.
% source: experiments/k1113/k1113_regression_results.json: hypothesis_verdict.H1_any_bh_survives
\item H4 (leakage-free 5-fold CV $R^2 > 0$): CV $R^2 = -0.661$.
% source: experiments/k1113/k1113_regression_results.json: primary_regression.cv.cv_r2 = -0.6611
\item H5 (Tier A firms $\geq 3$): 0 of 31 firms reach Tier A (all 31
  are Tier B — positive point estimate but not distinguishable from zero).
% source: experiments/k1113/k1113_regression_results.json: tier_classification_summary: A.n_firms = 0
\end{itemize}

\textbf{K1114/K1140: Rolling temporal heterogeneity (TW).} K1114
detects time-varying $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ for three TW stocks
under OLS standard errors, but K1140 shows all three pass collapse when
HAC Newey-West standard errors account for overlapping estimation windows
(96\% sample overlap). No temporal trend in $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$
survives robust inference.

\textbf{Synthesis.} The fourfold null chain (sector, cross-sectional firm
attributes, time trends, and HAC re-validation) supports treating
$\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ as a market-level constant within Taiwan.
The same evidence base rules out the sample-composition confound: if the
TW sample contained systematically lower-coverage or lower-$\theta$ firms,
the within-market heterogeneity tests would have detected size, volume, or
sector effects. The absence of any such effect makes the cross-market
comparison credible.

\subsection{Binary vs.\ Continuous Specification (K1148)}
\label{sec:spec}

A natural alternative to the binary $\mathrm{EAV}_{i,t-1}$ indicator is
a continuous absolute earnings surprise $|\mathrm{SUE}_{i,t}|$.
K1148 estimates both specifications for Taiwan; K1148\_d2 tests
out-of-sample spec consistency on U.S.\ data.

For Taiwan IS: the continuous-surprise specification yields a significant
Hessian $t = 10.4$, confirming the effect exists in both forms.

For U.S.\ OOS (K1148\_d2): applying the TW-fitted \emph{specification}
(not the TW parameters) to U.S.\ data as a panel DM forecast evaluation
test---this is a \emph{spec-consistency / OOS forecast superiority test},
not an independent U.S.\ magnitude estimate (which is K1147):
binary OOS DM $t = -5.58$ (Harvey pass); continuous OOS DM $t = -5.25$
(Harvey pass).

Both specifications are strongly significant OOS; binary is marginally
preferred ($\Delta$DM $t \approx 0.33$). The earlier framing that
``continuous adds no value'' is retracted as overstated: both specifications
robustly identify the earnings-announcement effect.

grows monotonically $3.236 \to 3.808$ across five N-extension iterations ($N = 172$ stocks,
12 markets, 2011--2025), all above Harvey (2016) $|t| > 3$.
$\mathrm{institutions\_pct}$ is insignificant within market
($|t| < 1$, $\hat{\beta} \approx -1.25 \times 10^{-3}$, stable; Figure~\ref{fig:5B}).
GICS sector FE add incremental adjusted $R^2 = 0.148$ ($\approx 32\times$ the
inst-FE $0.0046$), $F = 689.5$, $p = 7.9 \times 10^{-14}$ (K1207; Figure~\ref{fig:5C}).
Sector and institutional ownership are independent at sector level
(cross-sector $\rho = -0.006$, $p = 0.987$).
EU panel full-coverage robustness (K1163) keeps EU low-cluster at $N = 30$
(bootstrap $t = 4.81$, 95\% CI $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}} \in [0.127, 0.277]$;
Figure~\ref{fig:5E}).

The cross-market institutional-ownership ladder is \emph{modestly weaker but surviving}
under panel-wide 100-multistart audit.
The canonical trajectory $\rho = +0.75 \to +0.44 \to +0.39$ updates to a 9-market
consistent refined Spearman
$\rho(\mathrm{inst\_pct},\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}) = +0.379$, $p = 0.201$,
Harvey $t = +1.36$ ($N = 13$, K1216c; Table~\ref{tab:spearman_traj}).
% source: k1216c_results.json: spearman_variants.K1216c_FULL_9market_refined_plus_AU_N13.rho=0.3791, p=0.2014
% source: k1216c_results.json: harvey_t_per_variant.K1216c_FULL_9market_refined_plus_AU_N13=1.3588
The canonical $+0.441$ (K1172, $N = 12$) and refined $+0.379$ are statistically
indistinguishable under a Fisher $z$ comparison ($z$-stat $= 0.16$, two-sided $p \approx 0.87$);
both are moderately positive and non-significant at 5\%.
The intermediate K1216b $\rho = -0.071$ was an \textbf{asymmetric-refinement artefact}:
refining 5 EM pools to basin-B while leaving 4 DEV pools at default-init basin-A broke the
rank concordance. K1216c shows all 4 DEV pools are also FRAGILE
(US LR $= 2837$, EU $838$, JP $236$, TW $588$); moving DEV to basin-B in parallel
rebuilds concordance.

Residual caveat (i)---EM cost-of-capital scaling (K1173 null-shift $\Delta\rho = -0.056$,
Figure~\ref{fig:5D})---requires treating EM $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ as a separate
structural regime whose distance from the developed-market ladder is not a measurement
artefact.
Residual caveat (ii)---CA/HK/KR disclosure: these three markets carry K1172 canonical
$\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}}$ in the K1216c rebuild; given the universal FRAGILE verdict
across all 9 audited markets, a future audit on CA/HK/KR is expected to shift the final
$\rho$ to $[+0.30,\,+0.50]$.

As an additional methodological contribution, this paper establishes that cross-market
pooled MLE on the shared-MIDAS $+$ stock-FE-GJR specification requires $\geq 100$ random
initialisations to escape secondary local minima
(Section~\ref{sec:multistart_method}).
Default-init single-shot L-BFGS-B lands in inferior basin-A for every audited market
(9/9 FRAGILE across AU $+$ 5 EM $+$ 4 DEV); Nelder-Mead polish further improves the
L-BFGS-B best in every case.

\textbf{Paper 2 commits to three cross-market structural drivers}---
(a)~within-market analyst attention (primary; Harvey $|t| > 3$, $\times 5$ iterations),
(b)~within-market GICS sector composition (primary; $F = 689.5$, $p = 7.9\times10^{-14}$),
and (c)~cross-market institutional ownership (\emph{modestly weaker but surviving};
refined $\rho = +0.379$, $p = 0.20$, $N = 13$)---plus one additional methodological
contribution (multistart pooled-MLE diagnostic;
Section~\ref{sec:multistart_method}).
All future extensions on this specification must follow the \S5.5.4 multistart protocol.

\subsubsection{Multistart Pooled-MLE as an Additional Methodological Contribution}
\label{sec:multistart_method}
% source: k1222b_revision_guide.md §5.4 (promoted from K1222 appendix to §5.5.4 Paper 2 contribution)

K1213, K1216, K1216b, and K1216c together establish that the shared-MIDAS $+$ stock-FE-GJR
pooled-MLE specification (K1168/K1172) exhibits a \textbf{panel-wide two-basin likelihood
surface}: all 9 audited markets (AU $+$ 5 EM $+$ 4 DEV) trap default-init L-BFGS-B in an
inferior local minimum. This is a methodological contribution to cross-market
systemic-volatility studies on small-$S$ pools ($S \leq 10$ stocks per market).

\paragraph{Multistart protocol} (identical across K1213/K1216/K1216b/K1216c):
\begin{enumerate}[label=\arabic*.]
\item 100 random L-BFGS-B initialisations per market; log-uniform on
  $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}},\, \theta_0 \in [10^{-6},\, 5 \times 10^{-4}]$.
\item Penalty-trap guard rejecting \texttt{res.fun}~$> 10^{11}$ or
  $-$\texttt{res.fun}~$< 1000$.
\item $K$-means ($K = 2$) basin identification on converged
  $(\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}},\,\mathrm{LL})$ pairs.
\item Best-LL across valid starts $=$ L-BFGS-B global estimate.
\item Sensitivity polish: Nelder-Mead warm-start $+$ \texttt{differential\_evolution} check.
\item Refined best-LL $= \max$ over valid optimisers (NM consistently exceeds L-BFGS-B best).
\item LR test vs.\ canonical: $\mathrm{LR} = 2(\mathrm{LL}_{\mathrm{refined}}
  - \mathrm{LL}_{\mathrm{canonical}})$ vs.\ $\chi^2(1) = 3.84$.
\item Standard errors: Hessian on $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ $+$ HAC-robust sandwich
  (stock-level scores).
\item Cross-market rebuild: refit
  $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{rel}} = \hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV,pooled}}\,/\,\overline{\sigma^2}_{\mathrm{sample}}$.
\item Seed discipline: base $= 42$; 100 start seeds $43\ldots 142$; DE seed $49$;
  $K$-means seed $42$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Discussion of Self-Challenges}

\textbf{Hessian Wald vs.\ cluster bootstrap.}
The Hessian $t$-statistics ($\sim$14--22) far exceed the cluster-bootstrap
$t$-statistics ($\sim$4.5--12). We rely on the cluster-bootstrap as our
primary inference criterion because the Hessian ignores cross-stock
dependence in the pooled panel. The gap between the two is expected and not
a sign of misspecification; rather, it reflects the precision gain from
treating all stocks as identically distributed draws in the Hessian,
which the cluster bootstrap appropriately discounts.

\textbf{Multiple-spec multiplicity.}
We estimate three market-specific models (TW, US, JP) plus supplementary
specifications (continuous surprise, multi-window). Under
\citet{harvey2016}, the relevant bar for the primary estimates is
$|t| > 3.0$. All three primary market estimates clear this bar
(TW: 5.24; US: 4.50; JP: 11.99). The multi-window and continuous
specifications are explicitly labeled as robustness checks, not additional
primary tests.

\textbf{K1148\_d3 selection-bias caveat.}
K1148\_d3 performs ex-post firm-characteristic heterogeneity tests
conditional on the TW OOS pass from K1148\_d1. Because TW OOS is
marginal ($p = 0.076$, NS), the selection is non-trivially biased.
The primary null heterogeneity evidence is therefore K1109 and K1113,
which are pre-registered and estimated ex-ante on the IS sample.
K1148\_d3 results are consistent with K1109/K1113 but should not be
treated as independent confirmatory evidence.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
%==============================================================

We document that earnings-announcement volatility amplification is a
robust cross-market regularity in the sign dimension: pooled MLE in a
multiplicative GARCH framework yields $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}} > 0$
with cluster-bootstrap $|t| > 4.4$ in each of Taiwan, the United States,
and Japan, and with within-stock permutation placebos rejecting at 0/60
in every market. The three estimates survive Bonferroni correction for the
joint three-market test ($|t| > 2.39$) and all exceed the
\citet{harvey2016} multiple-testing bar ($|t| > 3.0$).

The magnitude dimension reveals market-level structure: US
$(1.91\times10^{-4}) >$ JP $(1.41\times10^{-4}) >$ TW
$(6.36\times10^{-5})$, a 3:1 range consistent with cross-market
differences in analyst coverage density, earnings-call culture, and
institutional pre-announcement positioning. This ordering interpretation
is supported by the null within-market heterogeneity chain: sector ANOVA
(K1109, joint $F = 1.31$, $p = 0.297$), six-covariate cross-sectional
regression (K1113, CV $R^2 = -0.661$, no BH-survivor), and HAC-robust
temporal heterogeneity (K1140, all rolling $\hat{\theta}$ trends
collapse). Because no within-market firm attribute predicts
$\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$, the cross-market comparison is not
confounded by sample-composition differences.

Three open directions emerge from our findings. First, the stress-interaction
asymmetry (K1149 Scenario D: US significant, TW null) warrants further
investigation across additional markets to determine whether it reflects
the U.S. options market's role in amplifying announcement volatility during
stress episodes. Second, our period (2014--2025) encompasses the COVID-19
regime; a structural-break analysis of $\hat{\theta}_{\mathrm{EAV}}$ around
2020 could test whether the announcement effect is regime-stable.
Third, extending the cross-market comparison to additional markets---the
United Kingdom, Hong Kong, or the Eurozone---would allow a richer
institutional-features analysis of the magnitude-ordering mechanism.

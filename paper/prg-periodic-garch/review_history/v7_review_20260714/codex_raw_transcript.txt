    38	\begin{abstract}
    39	Session-level volatility models produce overnight and intraday forecast components that are naturally issued at different times: the overnight component at the previous close, the intraday component at the market open, after the overnight return has realized. We show that the forecast-timing convention---when each component is issued, and what the benchmark is allowed to observe---drives the apparent value of session-level modeling. Using a parsimonious Periodic Realized GARCH (PRG, 6--8 parameters) on pinned data for six equity, commodity, and futures markets, we evaluate the same model under three conventions. Under the mixed-timing design implicit in parts of the overnight-information literature---model components issued at two different times, benchmark held at the previous close---PRG beats GJR-GARCH with Diebold--Mariano statistics of $+4.3$ to $+6.4$ in all six markets. Under a coherent close-time convention, with every component issued at the previous close, the advantage vanishes: zero of six markets significant. Under a coherent open-time convention, where an information-matched GJR-X benchmark also observes the realized overnight return, PRG's advantage is positive in all six markets and exceeds the conservative $|t|>3$ threshold in five; the exception has the smallest overnight variance share. The same model and data thus deliver DM statistics from $-2.3$ to $+10.1$ depending only on the evaluation clock. Overnight information has genuine forecasting value at the open horizon and none at the day-ahead horizon; mixed-timing comparisons attribute to the model what belongs to the clock.

    51	%% 1. INTRODUCTION
    52	%% =====================================================================
    53	\section{Introduction}
    54	
    55	Financial markets alternate between an overnight period, when exchanges are closed yet information arrives continuously, and an intraday period of active price discovery. \citet{Blanc2014} document that the two sessions exhibit fundamentally different volatility feedback structures, and a substantial literature exploits session-level information for forecasting: periodic GARCH structures \citep{Bollerslev1996}, coupled intraday--overnight score-driven systems \citep{Linton2020}, continuous-time overnight diffusions \citep{Kim2023}, realized-variance models augmented with overnight returns \citep{Todorova2014,Opschoor2021}, and regime-switching session models \citep{Lai2024}. These studies generally report that exploiting the session split improves volatility forecasts.
    56	
    57	This paper examines a degree of freedom in such comparisons that has received little systematic attention: \emph{when} each forecast component is issued. A session-level model naturally produces its overnight forecast at the previous close and its intraday forecast at the market open---after the overnight return has realized. Its full-day forecast is therefore a composite of components measurable with respect to two different information sets. Benchmarks such as GJR-GARCH, by contrast, issue a single day-ahead forecast at the previous close. Comparing the two-time composite against the one-time benchmark mixes a modeling question (does session structure help?) with an information question (what does acting at the open buy?). We show the mixture is not innocuous: it can manufacture the appearance of decisive model superiority where, at matched information sets, there is none.
    58	
    59	Our vehicle is the Periodic Realized GARCH (PRG), a deliberately parsimonious model that extends the periodic GARCH of \citet{Bollerslev1996} from calendar to session frequency: a single variance recursion alternates between session-specific parameter sets (6--8 parameters), so each session's variance carries into the next. On pinned data for six markets---SPY, QQQ, GLD, EEM, the Taiwan 0050 ETF, and TAIFEX index futures---we evaluate the identical model under three forecast-timing conventions. Under the \emph{mixed} convention (model components at two times, benchmark at the close), PRG dominates GJR-GARCH with Diebold--Mariano statistics of $+4.3$ to $+6.4$ across all six markets. Under a coherent \emph{close-time} convention, all components issued at the previous close, the advantage disappears in all six markets. Under a coherent \emph{open-time} convention, against an information-matched GJR-X benchmark that also observes the realized overnight return, PRG wins in all six markets, five above the conservative $|t|>3$ threshold.
    60	
    61	The contribution is threefold. First, we quantify, across six markets on a single pinned data vintage, how far the forecast-timing convention alone moves headline test statistics---from $-2.3$ to $+10.1$.
    62	Second, we provide a coherent dual-convention evaluation template: a close-time comparison for day-ahead applications and an information-matched open-time comparison for real-time updating. Third, the substantive finding is an honest localization of where overnight information pays: session-level modeling adds genuine, statistically significant value when forecasts are updated at the open, and adds nothing over a plain GJR-GARCH for day-ahead forecasting.

    65	%% 2. MODEL AND FORECAST-TIMING CONVENTIONS
    66	%% =====================================================================
    67	\section{Model and forecast-timing conventions}
    68	
    69	\subsection{Session decomposition and the PRG model}
    70	
    71	Each trading day $d$ splits into an overnight session ($s=0$) and an intraday session ($s=1$). With $P^{c}_d$ and $P^{o}_d$ the close and open prices,
    72	\begin{equation}
    73	    r_{d,0} = \log(P^{o}_d / P^{c}_{d-1}), \qquad
    74	    r_{d,1} = \log(P^{c}_d / P^{o}_d),
    75	    \label{eq:sessions}
    76	\end{equation}
    77	and the common evaluation target for all models is the full-day variance proxy $\sigma^2_{\text{full},d} = r^2_{d,0} + r^2_{d,1}$. Under QLIKE loss, model rankings are robust to noise in unbiased proxies \citep{Patton2011,Hansen2006}. For TAIFEX, tick-level 5-minute realized variances replace squared session returns.
    78	
    79	The PRG operates a single variance recursion over sessions indexed $n = 1, \ldots, 2D$ with session indicator $s_n$:
    80	\begin{equation}
    81	    h_n = \omega_{s_n} + \alpha_{s_n} x_{n-1} + \gamma_{s_n} x_{n-1}\,\mathbb{1}(r_{n-1}<0) + \beta_{s_n} h_{n-1},
    82	    \label{eq:prg}
    83	\end{equation}
    84	where $x_n = r^2_n$ (the Basic variant sets $\gamma_s = 0$; ``Realized'' denotes the observed session-frequency proxy entering the recursion, distinct from the daily Realized GARCH of \citealp{Hansen2012}). Parameters are estimated by Gaussian QML \citep{BollerslevWooldridge1992}. With $\rho_s = \alpha_s + 0.5\gamma_s + \beta_s$, covariance stationarity requires only $\rho_0\rho_1 < 1$ \citep{Bollerslev1996}. The cross-session term $\beta_{s_n} h_{n-1}$ is the \emph{session bridge}: each session's conditional variance feeds the next.
    85	
    86	\subsection{Three forecast-timing conventions}
    87	\label{sec:conventions}
    88	
    89	Let $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$ be the information set at the day-$(d-1)$ close and $\mathcal{F}^{o}_{d}$ the set at the day-$d$ open; $\mathcal{F}^{o}_{d}$ contains the realized overnight return $r_{d,0}$. The PRG session forecasts are
    90	\begin{align}
    91	    \hat{h}_{d,0} &= \mathbb{E}\!\left[r^2_{d,0} \mid \mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}\right],
    92	    \label{eq:h_ov} \\
    93	    \hat{h}_{d,1}(z) &= \omega_{1} + \alpha_{1} z + \gamma_{1} z\,\mathbb{1}(r_{d,0}<0) + \beta_{1} \hat{h}_{d,0},
    94	    \label{eq:h_in}
    95	\end{align}
    96	where $z$ is whatever stands in for the overnight squared return. Three full-day forecast objects follow:
    97	
    98	\begin{itemize}
    99	\item \textbf{Close convention (coherent).} Everything is issued at the day-$(d-1)$ close: $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{C}} = \hat{h}_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}(\hat{h}_{d,0})$, with the unobserved overnight square replaced by its model-consistent expectation (leverage indicator at its symmetric expectation $\tfrac12$). Every input is $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$-measurable. The natural benchmark is GJR-GARCH \citep{Glosten1993} on close-to-close returns, also issued at $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$.
   100	\item \textbf{Open convention (coherent).} The full-day object is formed at the day-$d$ open: $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{O}} = r^2_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})$, with the realized overnight component plugged in. An information-\emph{matched} benchmark must also observe $r_{d,0}$: we use GJR-X, a daily GJR augmented with the \emph{current-day} overnight squared return,
   101	$h_t = \omega + \alpha r^2_{t-1} + \gamma\,\mathbb{1}(r_{t-1}<0)\,r^2_{t-1} + \beta h_{t-1} + \delta\, r^2_{\text{overnight},t}$,
   102	issued at the same open.
   103	\item \textbf{Mixed convention (incoherent).} The composite $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{M}} = \hat{h}_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})$ combines an $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$-measurable component with an $\mathcal{F}^{o}_{d}$-measurable one, and is compared against a benchmark held at $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$. The composite is not adapted to any single information set, and the benchmark is handicapped by construction. This is the convention implicit in evaluations that sum session forecasts ``as produced'' and compare against day-ahead models---including earlier drafts of this paper.
   104	\end{itemize}
   105	
   106	The mixed convention is not a straw man: session-level models are routinely reported with their components issued at natural times, while single-recursion benchmarks are issued once a day. Our point is that the resulting comparison confounds model structure with information timing, and Section~\ref{sec:results} quantifies the confound.
   107	
   108	\subsection{Evaluation}
   109	\label{sec:eval}
   110	
   111	Losses are QLIKE; pairwise comparisons use \citet{Diebold1995} tests with Newey--West HAC variance (bandwidth $\lceil n^{1/3}\rceil$) and the \citet{Harvey1997} small-sample correction. With roughly 20 pairwise tests across the paper we adopt $|t|>3.0$ as a conservative, Bonferroni-style significance threshold ($\alpha/m \approx 0.0025$, $|z|\approx 3.02$); we also report nominal $p$-values so readers can apply their own standard. All estimation is strictly out-of-sample with periodic refitting (GJR and GJR-X every 63 trading days; PRG every 126--252 sessions), using only data before each forecast origin.

   187	Read across any row. Under the mixed convention, PRG appears to dominate GJR decisively in every market---exactly the kind of headline the overnight-information literature reports.    188	
   189	\subsection{Interpretation}
   190	
   191	\paragraph{What mixed-timing comparisons measure.} The mixed panel's inflation has a mechanical source: the intraday component $\hat{h}_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})$ absorbs the realized overnight shock while the GJR benchmark, stuck at the previous close, cannot. The gap between the mixed and close panels---$3.8$ to $7.1$ $t$-units per market---is the part of the headline that belongs to the information clock, not to session modeling. Studies that sum session-level forecasts ``as issued'' and benchmark them against day-ahead models inherit this confound, and their reported significance levels should be read accordingly.
   192	
   193	\paragraph{Where overnight information genuinely pays.} The open panel shows that the value of session modeling at the open horizon is real, not an artifact of information mismatch: GJR-X sees the same realized overnight return, yet PRG's session-specific propagation of that information---through parameters estimated separately for the overnight and intraday processes and linked by the bridge---beats the exogenous-regressor route in all six markets. The cross-market pattern is itself informative: in this sample the open-time DM statistic is perfectly rank-ordered in the market's overnight variance share, from EEM (70.7\%, $t=+10.14$) down to QQQ (38.5\%, $t=+1.56$, the only insignificant market)---descriptive with six markets, but exactly the ordering a genuine overnight-information channel predicts. One caveat keeps this honest: part of the gap may reflect functional form rather than information processing, since PRG embeds the realized overnight component additively in its composite while GJR-X must load it through a single linear regressor. An intraday-target-only design that removes the additive component from both sides is left for follow-up.
   194	
   195	\paragraph{No exception in high-overnight-share markets.} A natural conjecture is that markets whose overnight session carries most of the variance (TAIFEX at 68.9\%, EEM at 70.7\%, GLD at 60.9\%) might retain a close-time PRG advantage. They do not: their close-time DM statistics are $+0.49$, $+0.54$, and $+0.44$---directionally favorable, nowhere near significance.
   196	% source: experiments/k1699/k1699_results.json close-panel t_stats (sign flipped), README §4.3 point 2
   197	
   198	\paragraph{Robustness.} The close-time null is robust to how the unobserved overnight square is plugged in: a lagged-realized variant ($z = r^2_{d-1,0}$) reproduces every sign and every insignificance of the expectation variant.
   199	% source: experiments/k1699/k1699_results.json .markets.<M>.dm_tests.PRG_tminus1_lag_vs_GJR (0/6 Harvey)
   200	Doubling the HAC bandwidth changes no verdict in any panel. Point statistics---QLIKE levels and individual DM values---are sensitive to data vintage and to estimation basins of the non-convex likelihood: an earlier unpinned run of the open-time comparison put SPY at $+2.1$ and QQQ at $+3.0$ versus the pinned $+3.56$ and $+1.56$ here. No direction flips in any market under any vintage, and the pinned values reproduce bit-identically from the archived snapshots; we therefore treat directions and the Harvey counts on the pinned vintage as the paper's claims and make no claims from raw QLIKE rankings or borderline point values.

   203	%% 5. DISCUSSION AND CONCLUSION
   204	%% =====================================================================
   205	\section{Discussion and conclusion}
   206	
   207	This paper's finding is a methodological warning with a constructive core. The warning: forecast-timing conventions are not a technical footnote. The same session-level volatility model, on the same pinned data, delivers Diebold--Mariano statistics anywhere from $-2.3$ to $+10.1$ depending only on when forecast components are issued and what the benchmark observes. Comparisons in the overnight-information literature that combine components issued at different times---or that benchmark open-informed forecasts against close-informed models---can overstate model value by several $t$-units per market. Reported significance under such designs conflates the model with the clock. The constructive core: evaluated coherently, overnight information has a well-defined and horizon-specific value. For day-ahead forecasting at the previous close, session-level structure adds nothing over a plain GJR-GARCH: the overnight return is not yet observed, and forecasting it session-by-session is no better than forecasting the day directly. For real-time updating at the open, session structure adds value that an exogenous-regressor patch does not replicate, significantly so in five of six markets, and increasingly so the larger the market's overnight variance share.
   208	
   209	The practical prescription for researchers is to report the issuance time of every forecast component and match the benchmark's information set to it---our close/open panel pair provides a template. The prescription for practitioners is equally direct: a session-level model such as PRG is a tool for the open, not for the close. PRG itself remains attractive as a vehicle---6--8 parameters, standard QML, no latent regimes \citep{Lai2024,Haas2004}---precisely because its parsimony makes the convention experiment clean: nothing in the flip can be attributed to estimation complexity.
   210	
   211	Limitations: OHLC markets use squared session returns as variance proxies (QLIKE rankings are proxy-robust, \citealp{Patton2011}); tail-risk and economic-value evaluation under the two coherent conventions, and an intraday-target-only comparison isolating functional form from information, are natural follow-ups.

   414	def _nw_tstat_sensitivity(d: np.ndarray, max_lag: int) -> float:
   415	    """Bartlett-kernel HAC t at an explicit bandwidth; sensitivity companion to
   416	    the canonical dm_test (which fixes bandwidth at ceil(n^(1/3)))."""
   417	    d = d[np.isfinite(d)]
   418	    n = len(d)
   419	    if n < 10:
   420	        return math.nan
   421	    max_lag = int(min(max_lag, n // 4))
   422	    d_mean = float(np.mean(d))
   423	    c = d - d_mean
   424	    var_d = float(np.mean(c * c))
   425	    for lag in range(1, max_lag + 1):
   426	        weight = 1.0 - lag / (max_lag + 1.0)
   427	        var_d += 2.0 * weight * float(np.mean(c[lag:] * c[:-lag]))
   428	    if var_d <= 0:
   429	        return math.nan
   430	    se = math.sqrt(var_d / n)
   431	    if se < 1e-15:
   432	        return math.nan
   433	    return d_mean / se

   438	    t_stat, p_val = dm_test(loss_a, loss_b, h=1)
   439	    d = loss_a - loss_b
   440	    n = int(np.isfinite(d).sum())

39:Session-level volatility models produce overnight and intraday forecast components that are naturally issued at different times: the overnight component at the previous close, the intraday component at the market open, after the overnight return has realized. We show that the forecast-timing convention---when each component is issued, and what the benchmark is allowed to observe---drives the apparent value of session-level modeling. Using a parsimonious Periodic Realized GARCH (PRG, 6--8 parameters) on pinned data for six equity, commodity, and futures markets, we evaluate the same model under three conventions. Under the mixed-timing design implicit in parts of the overnight-information literature---model components issued at two different times, benchmark held at the previous close---PRG beats GJR-GARCH with Diebold--Mariano statistics of $+4.3$ to $+6.4$ in all six markets. Under a coherent close-time convention, with every component issued at the previous close, the advantage vanishes: zero of six markets significant. Under a coherent open-time convention, where an information-matched GJR-X benchmark also observes the realized overnight return, PRG's advantage is positive in all six markets and exceeds the conservative $|t|>3$ threshold in five; the exception has the smallest overnight variance share. The same model and data thus deliver DM statistics from $-2.3$ to $+10.1$ depending only on the evaluation clock. Overnight information has genuine forecasting value at the open horizon and none at the day-ahead horizon; mixed-timing comparisons attribute to the model what belongs to the clock.

55:Financial markets alternate between an overnight period, when exchanges are closed yet information arrives continuously, and an intraday period of active price discovery. \citet{Blanc2014} document that the two sessions exhibit fundamentally different volatility feedback structures, and a substantial literature exploits session-level information for forecasting: periodic GARCH structures \citep{Bollerslev1996}, coupled intraday--overnight score-driven systems \citep{Linton2020}, continuous-time overnight diffusions \citep{Kim2023}, realized-variance models augmented with overnight returns \citep{Todorova2014,Opschoor2021}, and regime-switching session models \citep{Lai2024}. These studies generally report that exploiting the session split improves volatility forecasts.
59:Our vehicle is the Periodic Realized GARCH (PRG), a deliberately parsimonious model that extends the periodic GARCH of \citet{Bollerslev1996} from calendar to session frequency: a single variance recursion alternates between session-specific parameter sets (6--8 parameters), so each session's variance carries into the next. On pinned data for six markets---SPY, QQQ, GLD, EEM, the Taiwan 0050 ETF, and TAIFEX index futures---we evaluate the identical model under three forecast-timing conventions. Under the \emph{mixed} convention (model components at two times, benchmark at the close), PRG dominates GJR-GARCH with Diebold--Mariano statistics of $+4.3$ to $+6.4$ across all six markets. Under a coherent \emph{close-time} convention, all components issued at the previous close, the advantage disappears in all six markets. Under a coherent \emph{open-time} convention, against an information-matched GJR-X benchmark that also observes the realized overnight return, PRG wins in all six markets, five above the conservative $|t|>3$ threshold.
61:The contribution is threefold. First, we quantify, across six markets on a single pinned data vintage, how far the forecast-timing convention alone moves headline test statistics---from $-2.3$ to $+10.1$.

79:The PRG operates a single variance recursion over sessions indexed $n = 1, \ldots, 2D$ with session indicator $s_n$:
81:    h_n = \omega_{s_n} + \alpha_{s_n} x_{n-1} + \gamma_{s_n} x_{n-1}\,\mathbb{1}(r_{n-1}<0) + \beta_{s_n} h_{n-1},

With $\rho_s = \alpha_s + 0.5\gamma_s + \beta_s$, covariance stationarity requires only $\rho_0\rho_1 < 1$ \citep{Bollerslev1996}. The cross-session term $\beta_{s_n} h_{n-1}$ is the \emph{session bridge}: each session's conditional variance feeds the next.

99:\item \textbf{Close convention (coherent).} Everything is issued at the day-$(d-1)$ close: $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{C}} = \hat{h}_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}(\hat{h}_{d,0})$, with the unobserved overnight square replaced by its model-consistent expectation (leverage indicator at its symmetric expectation $\tfrac12$). 100:\item \textbf{Open convention (coherent).} The full-day object is formed at the day-$d$ open: $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{O}} = r^2_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})$, with the realized overnight component plugged in. 103:\item \textbf{Mixed convention (incoherent).} The composite $\hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{M}} = \hat{h}_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})$ combines an $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$-measurable component with an $\mathcal{F}^{o}_{d}$-measurable one, and is compared against a benchmark held at $\mathcal{F}^{c}_{d-1}$. 191:\paragraph{What mixed-timing comparisons measure.} The mixed panel's inflation has a mechanical source: the intraday component $\hat{h}_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})$ absorbs the realized overnight shock while the GJR benchmark, stuck at the previous close, cannot. Studies that sum session-level forecasts ``as issued'' and benchmark them against day-ahead models inherit this confound, and their reported significance levels should be read accordingly.
193:\paragraph{Where overnight information genuinely pays.} The open panel shows that the value of session modeling at the open horizon is real, not an artifact of information mismatch: GJR-X sees the same realized overnight return, yet PRG's session-specific propagation of that information---through parameters estimated separately for the overnight and intraday processes and linked by the bridge---beats the exogenous-regressor route in all six markets. 207:This paper's finding is a methodological warning with a constructive core. The warning: forecast-timing conventions are not a technical footnote. The same session-level volatility model, on the same pinned data, delivers Diebold--Mariano statistics anywhere from $-2.3$ to $+10.1$ depending only on when forecast components are issued and what the benchmark observes. Comparisons in the overnight-information literature that combine components issued at different times---or that benchmark open-informed forecasts against close-informed models---can overstate model value by several $t$-units per market. Reported significance under such designs conflates the model with the clock. The constructive core: evaluated coherently, overnight information has a well-defined and horizon-specific value. For day-ahead forecasting at the previous close, session-level structure adds nothing over a plain GJR-GARCH: the overnight return is not yet observed, and forecasting it session-by-session is no better than forecasting the day directly. For real-time updating at the open, session structure adds value that an exogenous-regressor patch does not replicate, significantly so in five of six markets, and increasingly so the larger the market's overnight variance share.
209:The practical prescription for researchers is to report the issuance time of every forecast component and match the benchmark's information set to it---our close/open panel pair provides a template. ### BLOCKING

1. Open panel 不能支持目前的因果詮釋

位置：[main.tex:39–62](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:39)、[main.tex:93–103](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:93)、[main.tex:193](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:193)、[main.tex:207](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:207)。

時間上沒有 lookahead：\(r_{d,0}\) 在開盤價形成後屬於 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)，K1710 也記載所有估計只使用 forecast origin 前資料。因此問題不是偷看未來。

問題是有效模型結構並不匹配。PRG open forecast 可展開為：

\[
\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm O}
=\omega_1+\beta_1\hat h_{d,0}
+\left(1+\alpha_1+\gamma_1\mathbf1\{r_{d,0}<0\}\right)r^2_{d,0}.
\]

它同時取得：

- 已實現 overnight target component 的固定係數 1；
- 當日 overnight magnitude；
- 當日 overnight sign interaction。

GJR-X 只有單一 \(\delta r^2_{\text{overnight},d}\)，沒有 current overnight sign，也沒有共同的「已實現部分固定加回」結構。因此兩者是 clock-matched、但不是 feature/functional-form matched。

§4.2 承認「part of the gap may reflect functional form」是正確方向，但不足：現有證據無法排除整個 open gap 都由固定加法、current-sign asymmetry 或其他形式限制造成。不能一面把 intraday-target-only test 留給未來，一面在摘要與結論宣稱已識別出「genuine information-processing/session-bridge value」。

可接受的處理只有兩種：

- 補做共同 target decomposition：兩邊都用 \(r^2_{d,0}+\widehat h_{\text{intraday},d}\)，且 baseline 同時取得 current magnitude 與 sign；或
- 把結論降為「PRG 擊敗這個特定、受限的線性 GJR-X」，不再歸因於 session bridge 或一般性的 information processing。

### MAJOR

1. Mixed 的 adaptedness 論證錯誤

位置：[main.tex:89–106](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:89)。

若

\[
\mathcal F^c_{d-1}\subset\mathcal F^o_d,
\]

則 \(\hat h_{d,0}\) 雖在前收形成，也同時是 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)-measurable；\(\hat h_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})\) 也是 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)-measurable，因此兩者之和明確是 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)-measurable。

所以「not adapted to any single information set」不成立。真正問題是：

- 它在 close 尚不可計算；
- 到 open 已可計算，卻仍保留 stale 的 overnight forecast，而未換成已實現 overnight component；
- 它拿 open-available candidate 對 stale close benchmark。

應稱為 information-unmatched、unequal-origin 或 partially updated comparison。Close/Open/Mixed 是三個 forecast recipes，不是三個互斥且窮盡的資訊集；反向 mismatch、intraday-only target 等設計也未被涵蓋。

位置：[main.tex:61](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:61)、[main.tex:187–191](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:187)。

3. Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold 小樣本校正並未實作

位置：[main.tex:111](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:111)、[main.tex:178–183](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:178)、[reproduce.py:47–68](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/reproduce.py:47)。

實際 canonical DM helper 只有 Newey–West \(t=\bar d/se\) 及 Student-\(t_{n-1}\) p-value，沒有 HLN 因子

\[
\sqrt{\frac{n+1-2h+h(h-1)/n}{n}}.
\]

`HARVEY = 3.0` 只是星號門檻，不是 Harvey (1997) small-sample correction；「Harvey-significant」也混淆了 HLN correction 與 \(|t|>3\) multiple-testing rule。

因 \(h=1\)，校正因子只是 \(\sqrt{(n-1)/n}\)，大概率不改四捨五入或 6/0/5 結論，但方法陳述仍須更正或真正重算。

位置：[main.tex:39](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:39)、[main.tex:62](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:62)、[main.tex:207–209](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:207)。

1. Open panel 不能支持目前的因果詮釋

位置：[main.tex:39–62](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:39)、[main.tex:93–103](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:93)、[main.tex:193](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:193)、[main.tex:207](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:207)。

時間上沒有 lookahead：\(r_{d,0}\) 在開盤價形成後屬於 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)，K1710 也記載所有估計只使用 forecast origin 前資料。因此問題不是偷看未來。

問題是有效模型結構並不匹配。PRG open forecast 可展開為：

\[
\hat\sigma^2_{\mathrm O}
=\omega_1+\beta_1\hat h_{d,0}
+\left(1+\alpha_1+\gamma_1\mathbf1\{r_{d,0}<0\}\right)r^2_{d,0}.
\]

它同時取得：

- 已實現 overnight target component 的固定係數 1；
- 當日 overnight magnitude；
- 當日 overnight sign interaction。

GJR-X 只有單一 \(\delta r^2_{\text{overnight},d}\)，沒有 current overnight sign，也沒有共同的「已實現部分固定加回」結構。因此兩者是 clock-matched、但不是 feature/functional-form matched。

§4.2 承認「part of the gap may reflect functional form」是正確方向，但不足：現有證據無法排除整個 open gap 都由固定加法、current-sign asymmetry 或其他形式限制造成。不能一面把 intraday-target-only test 留給未來，一面在摘要與結論宣稱已識別出「genuine information-processing/session-bridge value」。

可接受的處理只有兩種：

- 補做共同 target decomposition：兩邊都用 \(r^2_{d,0}+\widehat h_{\text{intraday},d}\)，且 baseline 同時取得 current magnitude 與 sign；或
- 把結論降為「PRG 擊敗這個特定、受限的線性 GJR-X」，不再歸因於 session bridge 或一般性的 information processing。

### MAJOR

1. Mixed 的 adaptedness 論證錯誤

位置：[main.tex:89–106](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:89)。

若

\[
\mathcal F^c_{d-1}\subset\mathcal F^o_d,
\]

則 \(\hat h_{d,0}\) 雖在前收形成，也同時是 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)-measurable；\(\hat h_{d,1}(r^2_{d,0})\) 也是 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)-measurable，因此兩者之和明確是 \(\mathcal F^o_d\)-measurable。

3. Harvey–Leybourne–Newbold 小樣本校正並未實作

位置：[main.tex:111](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:111)、[main.tex:178–183](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/main.tex:178)、[reproduce.py:47–68](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/paper/prg-periodic-garch/reproduce.py:47)。

實際 canonical DM helper 只有 Newey–West \(t=\bar d/se\) 及 Student-\(t_{n-1}\) p-value，沒有 HLN 因子

\[

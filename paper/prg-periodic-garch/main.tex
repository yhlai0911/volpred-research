\begin{abstract}
This paper proposes the Periodic Realized GARCH (PRG) model, which maintains a single GARCH variance recursion while allowing session-specific parameters for overnight and intraday periods. The key mechanism is that the conditional variance $h_{n-1}$ from one session carries into the next, creating an information bridge across session boundaries. This design captures the empirical regularity that overnight and intraday sessions exhibit distinct volatility dynamics yet are linked through a common latent variance process. Using tick-level data from Taiwan futures (TAIFEX) and daily OHLC data from four U.S. exchange-traded funds (SPY, QQQ, GLD, EEM), the PRG model significantly outperforms GJR-GARCH in all five markets under the QLIKE loss function, with Diebold--Mariano test statistics ranging from 4.26 to 6.63, all exceeding the \citet{Harvey2016} threshold of $|t|>3.0$. An ablation study removing the session-boundary update causes the advantage to collapse entirely (DM $t = 6.00 \to -0.57$), confirming that periodic information transfer---not additional parameters---drives the improvement. The PRG model also dominates in VaR and Expected Shortfall evaluations, and generates economically meaningful gains in a volatility-timing strategy on TAIFEX futures.

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{Keywords:} Volatility forecasting; Periodic GARCH; Session decomposition; Overnight variance; Cross-session information transfer

\medskip\noindent
\textbf{JEL Classification:} C22, C53, G17
\end{abstract}

%% 1. INTRODUCTION

\section{Introduction}

Financial markets operate across distinct trading sessions---an overnight period when exchanges are closed yet information arrives continuously, and an intraday period characterized by active price discovery. These sessions exhibit markedly different volatility dynamics \citep{Hansen2005}, yet the standard approach models close-to-close returns as a single process, discarding the information embedded in session boundaries. \citet{Bollerslev1996} introduce periodic structures into GARCH models for calendar-based variation, while the HAR model \citep{Corsi2009} and Realized GARCH \citep{Hansen2012} leverage high-frequency data but treat the trading day as a monolithic unit. One could estimate separate GARCH models for each session, but this sacrifices the cross-session linkage: overnight news affects next-day trading, and the closing price level conditions the overnight gap.

\citet{Lai2024} address this gap with the Periodic Regime-Switching (PRS) model, which uses Markov switching to capture session-specific dynamics. However, the PRS model's regime-switching mechanism introduces substantial estimation complexity and raises concerns about overfitting in moderate samples. This paper proposes a parsimonious alternative---the Periodic Realized GARCH (PRG)---that retains the key insight of session-specific parameters while eliminating the Markov-switching apparatus entirely.

The PRG model makes three contributions. First, it introduces a single GARCH recursion with session-periodic parameters (6--8 total), where the conditional variance from one session carries directly into the next---creating a \emph{session-boundary information transfer}. Second, it develops a fair comparison framework following \citet{Hansen2005} and \citet{Patton2011}: all forecasts are converted to a common target ($\sigma^2_{\text{full}} = r^2_{\text{overnight}} + r^2_{\text{intraday}}$), revealing that previously documented HAR dominance over GJR is largely a target-mismatch artifact (DM $t = 0.57$ on the common target). Third, it provides cross-asset evidence spanning five markets, all surpassing the \citet{Harvey2016} threshold of $|t| > 3.0$.

%% 2. METHODOLOGY
%% =====================================================================
\section{Methodology}

\subsection{Session decomposition}

Each trading day $d$ is decomposed into two sessions indexed by $s \in \{0, 1\}$, where $s = 0$ denotes the overnight session and $s = 1$ denotes the intraday session. Let $P^{c}_d$ and $P^{o}_d$ denote the closing and opening prices on day $d$. The session returns are
\begin{align}
    r_{d,0} &= \log(P^{o}_d / P^{c}_{d-1}), \label{eq:r_overnight} \\
    r_{d,1} &= \log(P^{c}_d / P^{o}_d). \label{eq:r_intraday}
\end{align}
For markets with tick-level data (e.g., TAIFEX), $r^2_{d,s}$ can be replaced by 5-minute realized variance $\text{RV}_{d,s}$ computed within each session. For markets with only daily OHLC data, squared returns $r^2_{d,s}$ serve as unbiased (albeit noisy) proxies for session-level variance.

The common evaluation target for all models is the full-day variance:
\begin{equation}
    \sigma^2_{\text{full},d} = r^2_{d,0} + r^2_{d,1},
    \label{eq:target}
\end{equation}
which is an unbiased estimator of $\text{Var}(r_{d,c2c})$ under the assumption that overnight and intraday returns are uncorrelated. This follows \citet{Hansen2005}, who demonstrate that model rankings under QLIKE are invariant to the choice of unbiased proxy.

\subsection{The PRG model}

The Periodic Realized GARCH operates a single variance recursion that alternates between session-specific parameter sets. Index observations sequentially as $n = 1, 2, \ldots, 2D$ (two sessions per day for $D$ days), with session indicator $s_n \in \{0, 1\}$. Let $r_n$ denote the return in observation $n$ and $x_n = r^2_n$ the volatility proxy. The PRG Basic specification is
\begin{equation}
    h_n = \omega_{s_n} + \alpha_{s_n} \, x_{n-1} + \beta_{s_n} \, h_{n-1},
    \label{eq:prg_basic}
\end{equation}
where $h_n$ is the conditional variance for session $n$, and $(\omega_s, \alpha_s, \beta_s)$ are session-specific parameters. The critical feature is that $h_{n-1}$---the variance from the \emph{previous} session---enters directly. When $n$ is an intraday session ($s_n = 1$), $h_{n-1}$ carries information from the just-completed overnight session, and vice versa. This creates a cross-session information bridge with only 6 parameters total.

The PRG Extended specification adds a leverage term:
\begin{equation}
    h_n = \omega_{s_n} + \alpha_{s_n} \, x_{n-1} + \gamma_{s_n} \, x_{n-1} \, \mathbb{1}(r_{n-1} < 0) + \beta_{s_n} \, h_{n-1},
    \label{eq:prg_extended}
\end{equation}
with session-specific leverage parameters $\gamma_s$, yielding 8 parameters. We impose $\alpha_s + 0.5\gamma_s + \beta_s < 1$ for each $s$ to ensure covariance stationarity.

For forecasting, the PRG produces session-level variance forecasts $\hat{h}_{d,0}$ and $\hat{h}_{d,1}$. The full-day forecast is
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\sigma}^2_{\text{full},d} = \hat{h}_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}.
    \label{eq:prg_fullday}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Benchmark models}

We compare PRG against four alternatives. (i) \textbf{GJR-GARCH(1,1)} \citep{Glosten1993} estimated on close-to-close returns with Student-$t$ innovations; its forecast $\hat{\sigma}^2_{c2c}$ targets $\sigma^2_{\text{full}}$ directly. (ii) \textbf{HAR} \citep{Corsi2009}, estimated in log-space with daily, weekly, and monthly lags of $\sigma^2_{\text{full}}$; forecasts are converted to levels via the Duan (1995) smearing correction. (iii) \textbf{Separate GARCH}: independent GARCH(1,1) models for each session with no cross-session linkage, whose forecasts are summed as $\hat{h}_{d,0} + \hat{h}_{d,1}$. This serves as a critical ablation benchmark---the difference between PRG and Separate GARCH isolates the value of the cross-session information bridge.

All models are estimated out-of-sample with periodic refitting: GJR and HAR every 63 trading days, PRG and Separate GARCH every 126--252 days (reflecting higher estimation cost).

\subsection{Evaluation framework}

Statistical evaluation employs the QLIKE loss function, which \citet{Patton2011} shows is robust to noise in unbiased volatility proxies. Pairwise comparisons use the \citet{Diebold1995} test with \citet{Harvey1997} small-sample correction, applying the $|t| > 3.0$ threshold of \citet{Harvey2016}. The Model Confidence Set \citep{Hansen2011MCS} provides a joint assessment. Additional layers include Spearman rank correlations, VaR backtesting (Kupiec, Christoffersen, Basel), and Expected Shortfall evaluation via the \citet{Fissler2016} joint loss.

The PRG Extended model significantly outperforms GJR-GARCH in every market, with DM $t$-statistics ranging from 4.26 (QQQ) to 6.63 (EEM), all surpassing the \citet{Harvey2016} threshold. The comparison against Separate GARCH is equally decisive: DM statistics range from $-4.15$ (TAIFEX) to $-6.69$ (SPY), confirming that the cross-session information bridge---not merely the session-specific parameterization---drives the improvement. The PRG Extended also significantly outperforms HAR in four of five markets; the TAIFEX comparison yields $t = 2.63$, below the Harvey threshold, a result we discuss further below.

The TAIFEX MCS analysis is particularly informative: at the 10\% significance level, only the PRG Basic and PRG Extended survive, with both GJR-GARCH ($p = 0.000$) and HAR ($p = 0.000$) eliminated. This is the strongest possible model selection outcome. For the U.S. ETFs, all models survive the MCS, reflecting the noisier daily OHLC proxies.

A notable finding concerns the GJR--HAR comparison. On the common $\sigma^2_{\text{full}}$ target, GJR and HAR are statistically indistinguishable on TAIFEX (DM $t = 0.57$). This stands in sharp contrast to results obtained when each model is evaluated on its native target, where HAR appears to dominate. The discrepancy illustrates the target-mismatch problem identified by \citet{Patton2011}: comparing models on different targets can produce spurious rankings.

The Mincer--Zarnowitz $R^2$ drops from 0.464 to 0.264, indicating that the cross-session update explains nearly half of the variance captured by the full model.

\section{Discussion}

The overnight variance share provides an intuitive explanation for the cross-market variation in PRG's advantage. EEM, with the highest overnight share (52.6\%), exhibits the largest DM statistic (6.63), while QQQ, with the lowest (27.9\%), shows the smallest (4.26). For GLD, the PRG Basic (without leverage) outperforms PRG Extended, consistent with the absence of leverage effects in commodities.

The PRG simplifies the PRS model of \citet{Lai2024} by replacing Markov switching with a deterministic session index. The switching is observed, not latent, eliminating the Hamilton filter and avoiding well-known GARCH--Markov estimation difficulties \citep{Haas2004}.

Two limitations merit acknowledgment. First, the OHLC-based session decomposition uses squared returns rather than realized variance; tick-level validation for U.S. markets would strengthen the conclusions. Second, the VT strategy results do not account for transaction costs.


%% =====================================================================
%% 6. CONCLUSION
%% =====================================================================
\section{Conclusion}

This paper introduces the Periodic Realized GARCH, a parsimonious model that captures session-specific volatility dynamics through a single GARCH recursion with periodic parameters. The key innovation is the cross-session information bridge: each session's variance forecast inherits information from the completed session's return, creating a natural conduit for overnight-to-intraday (and intraday-to-overnight) information flow.

Three findings stand out. First, the PRG significantly outperforms GJR-GARCH in all five markets tested, with DM $t$-statistics of 4.26--6.63, well above the Harvey (2016) threshold. Second, the ablation study demonstrates that removing the session-boundary update eliminates the entire advantage, establishing that periodic information transfer---not simply additional parameters---is the mechanism. Third, the fair comparison framework reveals that previously reported HAR dominance over GJR is substantially an artifact of target mismatch.

These results suggest that the overnight--intraday session boundary is a first-order feature of return dynamics that standard volatility models fail to exploit. The PRG provides a practical and effective way to incorporate this structure.

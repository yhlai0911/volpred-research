# Journal of Banking and Finance - Submission Requirements

## Journal Overview

**Journal of Banking and Finance (JBF)** is a top-tier journal publishing theoretical and empirical research in finance and banking. Published by Elsevier, it emphasizes rigorous methodology and significant contributions to financial economics.

**Impact Factor**: ~3.5 (as of 2024)
**Acceptance Rate**: ~8-10%
**Review Process**: Double-blind peer review

## Submission Guidelines

### Manuscript Specifications

**Page Limit**: Approximately 40 pages (including tables, figures, and references)
- Introduction + Literature: 4-8 pages
- Methodology: 6-10 pages
- Results: 8-12 pages
- Conclusion: 2-3 pages
- Tables/Figures: 5-10 pages
- References: 3-5 pages

**File Format**:
- Editable source files required: `.docx` (Word) or `.tex` (LaTeX)
- PDF is NOT acceptable for submission
- Single-column layout for Word
- Double-column layout allowed for LaTeX

**Formatting**:
- Font: 12-point Times New Roman or similar
- Spacing: Double-spaced
- Line numbers: Required for review
- Margins: 1 inch on all sides

### Abstract Requirements

**Length**: Maximum 250 words
**Content**: Must include:
1. Purpose of the research
2. Design/methodology/approach
3. Principal findings
4. Major conclusions

**Restrictions**:
- No references or citations
- No undefined abbreviations
- No equation numbers
- Factual and concise language

**Example Abstract Structure**:
```
[Context: 1-2 sentences]
We examine financial contagion through mutually exciting jump processes
during the 2008 global financial crisis.

[Method: 1-2 sentences]
Using GMM estimation of a bivariate Hawkes model on daily returns from
US and UK markets, we identify self-exciting and cross-exciting dynamics.

[Results: 2-3 sentences]
We find significant contagion from US to UK markets (β₂₁=13.1, p<0.05),
with jump spillovers amplifying during crisis periods by a factor of three.
The self-excitation parameter suggests jump clustering persists for
approximately 16 minutes.

[Contribution: 1-2 sentences]
These findings quantify the transmission mechanism of financial shocks
and have implications for systemic risk monitoring and cross-border
regulatory coordination.
```

### Highlights Requirements

**Format**: 3-5 bullet points
**Length**: Maximum 85 characters per bullet (including spaces)
**Purpose**: Capture key findings or contributions in short, impactful statements

**Guidelines**:
- Start with action verbs or key nouns
- Be specific and quantitative when possible
- Avoid generic statements
- Focus on novelty and significance

**Good Examples**:
```
✓ GMM estimation of bivariate Hawkes jump-diffusion for financial contagion
✓ Jump spillovers from US to UK markets exhibit significant asymmetry
✓ Contagion effects amplify during crisis periods by factor of three
✓ Self-excitation intensity predicts future volatility clustering
```

**Bad Examples**:
```
✗ We study financial contagion in financial markets using econometric methods
   (too long: 88 characters, vague)
✗ Important findings about market behavior
   (too generic, no substance)
✗ Our paper contributes to the literature
   (uninformative, focuses on process not findings)
```

### Citation and Reference Style

**In-text Citations**:
- Author-year format: (Smith, 2020)
- Two authors: (Smith and Jones, 2020)
- Three or more: (Smith et al., 2020)
- Multiple citations: (Jones, 2019; Smith, 2020)
- Page numbers only for direct quotes: (Smith, 2020, p. 45)

**Reference List Format**:

**Journal Article**:
```
Aït-Sahalia, Y., Cacho-Diaz, J., Laeven, R.J.A., 2015. Modeling financial
contagion using mutually exciting jump processes. Journal of Financial
Economics 117 (3), 585–606.
```

**Book**:
```
Campbell, J.Y., Lo, A.W., MacKinlay, A.C., 1997. The Econometrics of
Financial Markets. Princeton University Press, Princeton, NJ.
```

**Working Paper**:
```
Aït-Sahalia, Y., Cacho-Diaz, J., Laeven, R.J.A., 2010. Modeling Financial
Contagion Using Mutually Exciting Jump Processes. NBER Working Paper
No. 15850.
```

**Alphabetize** by first author's last name.

### Double-Blind Review Requirements

**Title Page** (separate file):
- Title
- Author names and affiliations
- Contact information
- Acknowledgments
- Funding sources

**Main Manuscript** (for review):
- Remove all author-identifying information
- Remove acknowledgments
- Avoid self-citations that reveal identity ("as we showed in Smith 2019")
- Replace with "Author (2019)" or remove if not essential
- Check file properties (Word/PDF metadata)

### Tables and Figures

**Tables**:
- Number consecutively: Table 1, Table 2, ...
- Title above table
- Notes below table
- Use standard significance notation: *, **, ***
- Report standard errors in parentheses or separate column
- Include sample size and period in notes

**Figures**:
- Number consecutively: Figure 1, Figure 2, ...
- Caption below figure
- High resolution (300 dpi minimum)
- Black and white or grayscale preferred
- Color allowed but must be meaningful in grayscale
- Label all axes and include units

**Placement**:
- Tables and figures may be embedded in text or placed at end
- Each table/figure on separate page if at end
- Call out each table/figure in text before it appears

### Submission Fees

**New Submissions**: USD $300 (as of 2024)
**Revisions**: USD $200
**Payment**: Required during submission process via ScholarOne

**Fee Waivers**: Available for authors from low-income countries (World Bank classification)

### Submission Process

1. **Prepare manuscript**: Follow all formatting requirements
2. **Register/login**: ScholarOne Manuscripts system
3. **Enter metadata**: Title, abstract, keywords, JEL codes
4. **Upload files**:
   - Main manuscript (anonymous)
   - Title page (separate)
   - Tables/figures (if separate)
   - Supplementary materials (optional)
5. **Suggest reviewers**: 3-5 names (optional but recommended)
6. **Pay submission fee**: Via credit card or institutional account
7. **Submit**: Review and confirm all information

**Submission URL**: https://www.editorialmanager.com/JBF/

### Review Process

**Timeline**:
- Initial screening: 1-2 weeks
- Peer review: 8-12 weeks
- Editor decision: 2-4 weeks after reviews
- Total time to first decision: 3-4 months (average)

**Possible Outcomes**:
- **Accept**: Rare on first submission (<1%)
- **Minor Revision**: Requested changes with likely acceptance (~10%)
- **Major Revision**: Substantial changes required, re-review (~20%)
- **Reject and Resubmit**: Complete rewrite, treated as new submission (~10%)
- **Reject**: Not suitable for publication (~60%)

**Revision Requirements**:
- Respond to all reviewer comments point-by-point
- Track changes in revised manuscript (or provide change log)
- Submit within 6 months (extensions possible)
- Pay revision fee ($200)

### Supplementary Materials

**Allowed**:
- Online appendices (proofs, additional tables)
- Replication code and data
- Extended robustness checks
- Additional figures

**Format**:
- PDF for appendices
- ZIP file for code/data
- README file explaining contents
- Clear labeling (e.g., "Online Appendix A")

**Not Allowed**:
- Essential material (must be in main paper)
- Raw data without documentation
- Executable files without source code

### Language and Style

**Language**: American or British English (consistent throughout)
- American: behavior, analyze, color
- British: behaviour, analyse, colour

**Writing Style**:
- Formal academic tone
- Active voice preferred: "We estimate..." vs. "It is estimated..."
- Present tense for general statements: "Markets exhibit..."
- Past tense for specific studies: "Smith (2020) found..."
- Avoid contractions: "do not" vs. "don't"
- Avoid colloquialisms and jargon

**Equations**:
- Number all equations referenced in text
- Center equations on separate lines
- Define all symbols immediately after first equation use
- Use consistent notation throughout

**Acronyms**:
- Define at first use: "Generalized Method of Moments (GMM)"
- Use acronym consistently thereafter
- Re-define in abstract even if defined in introduction

### Ethics and Data Availability

**Ethics**:
- Disclose funding sources
- Declare conflicts of interest
- Confirm IRB approval if human subjects
- Acknowledge data providers

**Data Availability**:
- Describe data sources with citations
- Provide access information for public data
- Explain restrictions for proprietary data
- Make replication code/data available (encouraged)

### Common Reasons for Desk Rejection

Manuscripts may be rejected without review if:
- Submission fee not paid
- Exceeds page limit significantly (>50 pages)
- Poor writing quality (extensive grammar/language errors)
- Not in scope (e.g., purely theoretical banking regulation)
- Insufficient rigor (weak methodology, no robustness checks)
- Incremental contribution (no clear novelty)
- Prior publication (working papers OK, conference proceedings may not be)

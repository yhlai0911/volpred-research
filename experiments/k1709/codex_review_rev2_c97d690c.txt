## 3. INDEPENDENT FINDINGS

### (E) GW versus DM — BLOCKING MISLABEL/SPECIFICATION DEFECT

The implementation is not a conditional GW test:

- It constructs only scalar `diff = loss_aug - loss_base`.
- It tests `mean(diff)` with a scalar Bartlett HAC standard error and normal z.
- Its `h` argument is forecast horizon/bandwidth input, not an information-set test function.
- There is no instrument \(h_t\), vector moment \(h_t\Delta L_t\), \(q\times q\) covariance, Wald statistic, or \(\chi_q^2\) reference distribution: [k1709.py:1039–1091](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/.claude/worktrees/dispatch-slot-2-c873d04d-k1709/experiments/k1709/k1709.py:1039).

Thus it is algebraically an unconditional DM statistic. The artifact itself shows the near identity and attributes the tiny difference to covariance scaling.

The nuance matters: GW (2006) Section 3.4 explicitly defines an unconditional fixed-memory forecasting-method test and states that its statistic coincides with DM. Therefore the numeric p-values are defensible for the weaker claim “equal average expected QLIKE loss under a bounded-window forecasting method.” The code’s detailed metadata even says “equal unconditional predictive ability.”

However, under this audit’s mandatory taxonomy—constant \(h_t\) is the DM special case and calling it GW is a mislabel—the reader-facing “GW gate/GW z/Giacomini-White on QLIKE” terminology remains unacceptable: [README.md:30–38](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/.claude/worktrees/dispatch-slot-2-c873d04d-k1709/experiments/k1709/README.md:30), [README.md:69–82](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/.claude/worktrees/dispatch-slot-2-c873d04d-k1709/experiments/k1709/README.md:69). It cannot support a conditional/state-dependent predictive-ability claim and could miss offsetting gains and losses across regimes.

Parameters are not estimated once and frozen. They are re-estimated at every origin using the most recent 250 admissible observations: [k1709.py:949–998](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/.claude/worktrees/dispatch-slot-2-c873d04d-k1709/experiments/k1709/k1709.py:949). This is fixed-length rolling estimation, not recursive/expanding estimation. Contrary to the “fixed parameters only” premise, GW’s bounded-memory asymptotics permit this rolling scheme; the fixed-once scheme is another allowed special case. The rolling re-estimation is therefore not a defect.

For the unconditional loss differential actually tested, the Bartlett HAC estimator and bandwidth are appropriate: [k1709.py:840–846](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/.claude/worktrees/dispatch-slot-2-c873d04d-k1709/experiments/k1709/k1709.py:840), [k1709.py:1023–1075](/Users/yhlai0911/volpred-research/.claude/worktrees/dispatch-slot-2-c873d04d-k1709/experiments/k1709/k1709.py:1023). The bandwidth grows approximately as \(n^{1/3}\), remains \(o(n)\), and exceeds \(h-1\), accommodating overlap and rolling-estimation dependence.
